## Wiring: the part everyone gets stuck on

A true PoE doorbell ignores your old low-voltage doorbell transformer and takes both power and data over a single Ethernet cable. That leaves you with two clean paths depending on what's inside your wall:

1. **Full PoE run (Ethernet to the door).** Pull Cat6 to the door frame, plug the other end into a PoE switch port or $15 PoE injector, and you're done. One cable delivers power and rock-solid wired data, with zero Wi-Fi dropouts when someone walks up to the porch. Most front doors share a stud bay or sill plate with an unfinished basement, crawlspace, or attic, making a 2-meter fish easier than it looks. Both the [Reolink Doorbell PoE](/go/reolink-doorbell-poe/) and the [Aqara G400](/go/aqara-doorbell-g400/) support PoE.
2. **Existing 2-wire doorbell transformer (no Ethernet run).** If brick or concrete makes pulling Cat6 impossible, the PoE-only Reolink is the wrong SKU. Either use the [Aqara Doorbell G400](/go/aqara-doorbell-g400/) (which accepts 8–24VAC/DC from your existing doorbell wires and falls back to Wi-Fi for video) or buy Reolink's separate Wi-Fi doorbell model, which powers off 12–24VAC transformer wires.

<Notice type="info" title="Mechanical chime reality check">
Unlike Ring or Amcrest doorbells, the **Reolink Video Doorbell does not trigger your existing in-wall mechanical chime** (even on the Wi-Fi version, you bypass the old chime box). Instead, Reolink **includes one plug-in wireless Chime V2 in the box** that pairs over RF and rings instantly even if Home Assistant or your network is rebooting. If you have a multi-story house, you can pair up to five [Reolink Chime Plus](/go/reolink-chime-plus/) ($24.99) units to one doorbell, or ring any HomePod/Sonos/ESPHome speaker through a Home Assistant automation.
</Notice>

## What a doorbell needs for Home Assistant

- **RTSP and/or ONVIF.** Frigate and HA's camera integrations need a local video feed; cloud-only doorbells (Ring, Nest) are dead ends for self-hosting.
- **A local button-press event.** The doorbell *press* needs to reach HA as a binary sensor or event in milliseconds so automations can flash lights, pop up a camera card on a wall tablet, or announce on smart speakers. Reolink exposes it locally via HA's native Reolink integration; Aqara exposes it via HomeKit Device / Matter / Aqara integrations.
- **Local storage.** A microSD slot in the doorbell itself, Frigate recording, or a local NVR, with zero monthly fees.
- **Person/package detection.** On-device detection gives HA immediate `Visitor` and `Person` binary sensors without waiting for a separate detector pipeline.

## Wiring it into Frigate and Home Assistant

The Reolink PoE doorbell exposes standard H.264 RTSP main and sub streams (`2560×1920` main and `640×480` 4:3 sub), so the `go2rtc` + detect/record split from the [Frigate camera guide](/best-cameras-frigate-home-assistant/) applies directly. Enable RTSP and ONVIF in the Reolink client (**Network > Advanced > Server Settings**) first:

```yaml
go2rtc:
  streams:
    doorbell_main: rtsp://admin:password@192.168.30.12:554/h264Preview_01_main
    doorbell_sub:  rtsp://admin:password@192.168.30.12:554/h264Preview_01_sub

cameras:
  doorbell:
    ffmpeg:
      inputs:
        - path: rtsp://127.0.0.1:8554/doorbell_sub
          input_args: preset-rtsp-restream
          roles: [detect]
        - path: rtsp://127.0.0.1:8554/doorbell_main
          input_args: preset-rtsp-restream
          roles: [record]
    detect:
      width: 640
      height: 480
      fps: 5
```

For two-way talk on a Home Assistant dashboard (via the `advanced-camera-card` / WebRTC), `go2rtc` also supports Reolink's backchannel audio. Meanwhile, the physical button press arrives in HA instantaneously via the native Reolink integration as `binary_sensor.doorbell_visitor`, which makes a reliable trigger for: press -> snapshot notification to phones + TTS announcement on smart speakers. All local, no cloud.

On the network side, put the doorbell on your **camera VLAN** with no internet access (and remember that a port on the exterior wall of your house is untrusted physical access, so lock that switch port to the isolated camera VLAN). The [2.5GbE switch guide](/best-2-5-10gbe-switch-homelab/) covers PoE switches; any standard 802.3af port runs this doorbell at ~5–8W.

## FAQ

<Accordion label="Do PoE doorbells work without the internet?" group="faq" expanded="true">
Yes. The Reolink PoE doorbell records locally to microSD, rings its bundled wireless chime over RF, and streams RTSP/ONVIF + visitor events to Home Assistant and Frigate entirely on your LAN. On a VLAN with internet access blocked, it keeps working without a hitch; you simply use Home Assistant companion app notifications instead of Reolink's cloud push notifications.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="Can a PoE doorbell use my existing 2-wire doorbell wiring?" group="faq">
The Reolink Video Doorbell PoE cannot: it requires an Ethernet cable for both power (802.3af PoE) and data. If pulling Ethernet to the door frame is impossible, either buy the [Aqara Doorbell G400](/go/aqara-doorbell-g400/) (which supports both PoE and 8–24V two-wire transformer power with Wi-Fi) or choose Reolink's separate Wi-Fi doorbell model.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="Reolink doorbell vs Aqara G400: which is better for Home Assistant?" group="faq">
Pick the Reolink Video Doorbell PoE if you can run Ethernet and want the cleanest native Home Assistant + Frigate integration with zero cloud onboarding. Pick the Aqara G400 if you also use Apple HomeKit (HomePod chimes and Apple TV pop-ups) or need to power the doorbell from existing low-voltage doorbell wires.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="What about UniFi or Ring PoE doorbells?" group="faq">
