## Factors to consider when choosing a mini PC for a home server

### CPU and NPU: TOPS numbers are misleading

Every AMD marketing slide leads with TOPS numbers. Here's the reality: there's a difference between **NPU TOPS** and **platform TOPS** (which adds CPU + GPU + NPU together).

| Chip | NPU TOPS | Platform TOPS |
|------|----------|---------------|
| Ryzen AI 9 365 | 50 | 73 |
| Ryzen AI 9 HX 370 | 50 | 80 |
| Ryzen AI 9 HX 470 | 55 | 86 |
| Ryzen AI Max+ 395 | 50 | 126 |

The number that matters for local LLMs is **memory bandwidth + capacity**, not NPU TOPS. Ollama and llama.cpp use GPU compute paths, not XDNA NPUs. The EVO-X2 runs DeepSeek 32B at usable speed because of its 256-bit LPDDR5X-8000 memory (~256GB/s bandwidth), not the 50 TOPS NPU.

N-series chips (N150, N350, N355) have no NPU at all. That's fine. They're file servers and transcode boxes, not AI machines.

### Memory and storage: buy RAM-first in 2026

<Notice type="warning" title="DDR5 prices are elevated">
DDR5 RAM prices are up sharply in 2026. Prefer boxes with RAM pre-installed or barebones + RAM you already own. SO-DIMM boxes give you upgrade flexibility.
</Notice>

Guidance tiers:

- **16GB**: Fine for basic file serving, Docker containers, light Home Assistant use
- **32GB+**: Needed for AI workloads, multiple VMs, [Proxmox](/best-mini-pc-proxmox/) with several guests
- **64GB+**: Serious local LLM work, heavy virtualization, development environments

Storage-wise, PCIe 4.0 NVMe SSDs with 7000MB/s+ speeds are standard. Look for boxes with multiple M.2 slots. The AI X1 Pro has three, the MS-A2 has three, and the NUC 15 Pro has dual bays. When you need to expand later, see [how to add a new drive to an Ubuntu LVM volume](/add-new-drive-lvm/).

### Networking: 2.5GbE is standard, 10GbE is now affordable

2.5GbE is commoditized across all tiers. Even $130 N150 boxes include it. For a single-server homelab running Docker containers and media streaming, 2.5GbE is plenty.

10GbE now appears at $669-$1,299. It matters for NAS/Ceph/NFS-heavy setups where you're moving large volumes of data between machines. The MINISFORUM MS-A2 offers dual SFP+ 10G ports at $799 barebones, and the MS-02 goes to 25GbE.

One criticism: the GMKtec EVO-X2 at ~$2,000 still only has 2.5GbE (Realtek RTL8125BG). If you're spending that much and need fast networking, the MS-A2 is the better pick.

### Thermals, noise, and 24/7 reliability

For always-on server use, check these BIOS settings before committing:

- **Auto Power On** after AC loss (essential for unattended servers)
- **Wake-on-LAN** for remote management
- **Fan curve**: set to quiet/balanced, not performance
- **Headless boot**: confirm the box boots without a monitor attached

Noise matters if the box lives in your living space. The EVO-X2 fans are loud under load (community-reported; there's a P-mode button). The AI X1 Pro hits 45dB in performance mode. N150 boxes are effectively silent.

### ARM vs x86: which architecture for your homelab?

<Tabs>
<Tab name="ARM">
ARM systems excel at efficiency and always-on services:

- **Mac mini M6** ($899): 2nm chip, 10-15W idle, 2.5GbE standard, macOS ecosystem. Best for developers already in the Apple world.
- **MINISFORUM MS-R1** ($504): CIX CP8180, dual 10GbE, ECC memory, PCIe x8. The first real non-Apple ARM server buy.
- **Raspberry Pi 5**: PassMark ~4,400. Fine for light duties but outclassed by N150 boxes at similar cost.

ARM pros: low power, excellent efficiency, great for always-on services.
ARM cons: limited virtualization support (no nested KVM on Apple Silicon), some Docker images are x86-only, smaller ecosystem.
</Tab>
<Tab name="x86">
x86 systems offer maximum compatibility:

- Everything from N150 budget boxes to EVO-X2 flagships runs x86.
- Full virtualization support (KVM, Proxmox, ESXi).
- Every Docker image, every server application works out of the box.
- Intel QuickSync for hardware transcoding on N-series and Core Ultra.

x86 pros: universal software compatibility, mature virtualization, hardware transcoding.
x86 cons: higher power draw at the high end, more heat.
</Tab>
</Tabs>

For most home server users, modern x86 systems offer the best compatibility. ARM systems like the Mac mini M6 or MS-R1 are excellent for efficiency-focused always-on services. See [our ARM vs x86 VPS benchmark comparison](/arm-vs-x86-vps-server-benchmarks/) for real performance data.

## Common failure modes and gotchas

<Accordion label="N-series RAM ceiling" group="gotchas">
N150, N350, and N355 all have a 16GB official RAM ceiling. Single-channel memory only. 32GB works unofficially on many boards, but check your specific model before buying RAM. If you need 32GB+ guaranteed, skip N-series entirely.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="No AV1 encode on N-series" group="gotchas">
N150, N350, and N355 can decode AV1 but cannot encode it. This matters for Jellyfin remote streaming — if your clients support AV1, you'd want to transcode to AV1 to save bandwidth. N-series can't do that. AMD Ryzen AI boxes can.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="Fan noise under load" group="gotchas">
The EVO-X2 is loud under load (community-reported; there's a P-mode button for performance). The AI X1 Pro hits 45dB in performance mode. The MS-S1 Max has no barebones option. If noise matters, N150/N350 fanless boxes or the NUC 15 Pro+ (1.2x noise reduction) are better choices.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="Amazon listing SoC drift" group="gotchas">
The Beelink SER9 Pro ships with three different SoCs (Ryzen AI 9 365, Ryzen AI 9 HX 370, Ryzen 7 H255) at similar prices. Community reports say the HX 370 version is the same price, making the 365 a worse buy. Always confirm which processor is in the listing before clicking buy.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="EVO-X2 networking mismatch" group="gotchas">
The GMKtec EVO-X2 costs ~$2,000 but only has 2.5GbE (Realtek RTL8125BG). If you need 10GbE at this price point, the MINISFORUM MS-A2 at $799 barebones with dual SFP+ 10G is the better pick. The EVO-X2's strength is its 128GB LPDDR5X and iGPU, not networking.
</Accordion>

## Quick FAQ

<Accordion label="Can mini PCs run AI locally?" group="faq" expanded="true">
Yes, but the NPU TOPS number is misleading. For local LLMs, memory bandwidth + capacity matters more than NPU TOPS. The EVO-X2's 128GB LPDDR5X at ~256GB/s is what runs DeepSeek 32B at usable speed, not the 50 TOPS NPU. Ollama and llama.cpp use GPU compute paths, not XDNA NPUs. For lighter AI tasks, the Ryzen AI 9 HX 370 with 64GB DDR5 handles most models up to 13B parameters.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="How much RAM do I need?" group="faq">
16GB for basic file serving and Docker containers. 32GB+ for AI workloads and multiple VMs. 64GB+ for serious local LLM work and heavy virtualization. DDR5 prices are elevated in 2026 — buy RAM-forward (get boxes with RAM pre-installed) or buy barebones + RAM you already own.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="Are mini PCs upgradeable?" group="faq">
Depends on the model. SO-DIMM boxes (AI X1 Pro up to 128GB, MS-A2 up to 96GB, NUC 15 Pro up to 96GB, SER10 MAX) are upgradeable. Soldered LPDDR5X boxes (EVO-X2 max 128GB, SER9 Pro 32GB, Mac mini) — what you buy is what you get. Always check before purchasing if you think you'll need more RAM later.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="x86 or ARM for a home server?" group="faq">
x86 for maximum compatibility and virtualization support. Every Docker image and server application works. ARM (Mac mini M6, MS-R1) for efficiency and always-on services — lower power draw, but limited virtualization (no nested KVM on Apple Silicon) and some x86-only Docker images.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="What networking do I need?" group="faq">
2.5GbE is standard on all 2026 mini PCs and enough for most homelabs. 10GbE matters for NAS/Ceph/NFS-heavy setups where you're moving large data volumes between machines. 10GbE is now available from $669 (SER9 Max). The MS-02 offers 25GbE at $1,199 barebones.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="Does AV1 encoding matter?" group="faq">
Only if you remote-stream via Jellyfin/Plex to AV1-capable clients. AV1 encoding saves bandwidth significantly. N-series chips (N150, N350, N355) decode AV1 but cannot encode it. AMD Ryzen AI boxes can encode AV1. If most of your streaming is local or to H.265-capable clients, this doesn't matter.
</Accordion>
