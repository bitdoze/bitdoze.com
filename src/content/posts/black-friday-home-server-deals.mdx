## How to spot a real deal

Two tricks sellers run every November:

- **Inflated list prices.** The Anker Prime TB5 dock shows a $445.98 "list price" and a -22% badge — but its real street price has been ~$350–400 all year. The discount is smaller than it looks.
- **Merged listings.** Budget mini PCs (especially GMKtec) share one Amazon page across a dozen CPU variants. Confirm the selected configuration in the dropdown before checkout — the price you see may belong to a different model.

Paste the product URL into a price-history tracker (camelcamelcamel, Keepa) and compare against the baseline in the tables below.

## What to expect (and when to buy)

- **Oct 6–7 (Prime Big Deal Days):** Amazon usually discounts its own bestsellers early. Last year the Mac mini hit $469 and NAS boxes saw their first real cuts. If a product in the tables hits its target in October, buy it — it rarely goes lower in November.
- **Nov 27–30 (BF/CM):** The biggest cuts land on Amazon-choice SKUs and last-gen models. Premium docks (CalDigit) typically see $30–50 off, not 40%.
- **Watch stock, not just price.** The DXP4800 Plus was down to one unit at last check. NAS and mini PC inventory thins out fast in deal weeks — a good price with no stock is worse than a decent price today.

## FAQ

<Accordion label="Are Black Friday prices actually the lowest of the year?" group="faq">
For PC hardware and peripherals, usually yes — or within a few dollars of it. The exceptions are products launched in Q4 (first discounts come in spring) and items with inflated list prices where the "deal" is fake. The baselines above are the reference points.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="Should I wait for Black Friday or buy during Prime Big Deal Days?" group="faq">
If an item hits its target price during the October event, buy it. October discounts on Amazon bestsellers (the CP1500 UPS, mainstream NAS boxes) typically repeat — not beat — in November, and stock risk is real.
</Accordion>
