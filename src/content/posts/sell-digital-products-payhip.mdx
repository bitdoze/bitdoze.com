Physical products need inventory, shipping labels, and returns management. Digital products need a file and a way to collect payment. That's about it.

The margins are hard to ignore. You create the product once and sell it indefinitely. No per-unit manufacturing cost, no warehouse, no tracking numbers. A $15 ebook costs you the same whether you sell 10 copies or 10,000.

Before touching any platform, figure out what you're going to sell. The best digital products solve a specific problem or save someone time.

Ask yourself: What do people ask me for help with? What do I know that took me years to learn? Is there a shortcut I can package?

If you're a photographer, that might be presets. If you're a developer, maybe code snippets or boilerplates. If you're a teacher, a course makes obvious sense. Don't overthink it. Your first product doesn't have to be perfect. It needs to exist.

### Step 4: Add your first product

Click "Add Product" and choose your product type. Payhip lets you sell five different kinds of products, and they all work under the same account:

**Digital downloads** are the bread and butter. Ebooks, templates, presets, software, music, fonts, stock photos, anything that's a file. You upload it, set a price, and Payhip handles delivery after purchase. Buyers get instant access to a download page, plus an email with the link. There's a 5GB limit per file, but no cap on total storage or bandwidth.

For digital downloads specifically, you can set up pay-what-you-want pricing with a minimum amount. This works well for resource bundles where buyers feel good paying more. Payhip generates software license keys automatically if you're selling software, and since 2026 you can also upload pregenerated license keys (your own serial numbers) alongside the auto-generated ones. For PDF files, there's a PDF stamping feature that prints the buyer's purchase details on every page, discouraging unauthorized sharing.

You can limit download attempts too. By default, buyers get 3 download attempts per file, which you can adjust.

**Online courses** are where things get interesting if you're a teacher or expert in something. Payhip hosts full courses with video lessons, text content, quizzes, assignments, surveys, and downloadable files. You can drip content over time (so students unlock new lessons on a schedule) and issue completion certificates. Students get their own accounts to track progress.

For video, you have two options. Self-host through YouTube, Vimeo, or Wistia embeds for free, or use Payhip's own video hosting for $5/month. The paid video hosting (powered by Cloudflare Stream) includes 16 hours of video storage and per-video analytics like minutes watched. If your course outgrows 16 hours, the next tier is $10/month for 32 hours. For most solo course creators, the $5 plan is plenty.

**Coaching** products let you sell one-on-one sessions. You connect a calendar tool (Zoom, Calendly, Skype, Google Meet) and Payhip handles the booking and payment. Clients buy a session, pick a time slot, and show up. If you're a consultant, therapist, tutor, or any kind of freelancer selling your time, this removes the back-and-forth scheduling headache.

**Memberships** let you charge customers on a recurring basis, either monthly or yearly. Members get access to exclusive files, content, or a membership group that you manage. It's recurring revenue without building a separate membership site. You control what members see and can add or remove content over time.

**Physical products** are also supported if you want to mix digital and tangible goods. Payhip handles inventory tracking, order fulfillment, and shipping information. I personally stick to digital, but some sellers bundle a physical item (like a printed workbook) alongside their digital course.

## Tax compliance: what Payhip handles for you

This is one of Payhip's strongest selling points, and it got significantly better in 2026.

<Notice type="info" title="Tax handled automatically">
  Payhip automatically calculates, collects, and remits EU/UK digital VAT, US sales tax (as a marketplace facilitator, since July 1, 2026), and Canadian GST/HST/PST (as a digital platform operator, since July 1, 2026). You don't need to register for VAT in each EU country or file US state returns yourself.
</Notice>

For context: in the EU and UK, Payhip acts as the reseller of your digital products. They calculate the correct VAT rate based on the buyer's location, charge it, and remit it to the tax authorities. You don't touch it.

For the US, Payhip became a marketplace facilitator on July 1, 2026. That means they handle sales tax collection and remittance on your behalf in states that tax digital products. Not every US state taxes digital goods, and Payhip's per-product "Customize product tax category" option (under Advanced settings when adding or editing a product) lets you flag what kind of product you're selling so the right rules apply.

For Canada, Payhip became a digital platform operator on the same date, covering GST/HST and PST.

<Notice type="warning" title="Quebec QST is still yours">
  If you're selling to customers in Quebec and you're obliged to register for QST, you still need to report, collect, and remit QST yourself. Payhip's Canadian tax handling does not cover Quebec's provincial sales tax.
</Notice>

You can choose whether tax gets added on top of your listed price or included in it (Tax Settings in your dashboard). If you're comparing platforms, this automatic tax handling alone is worth the platform fee. Many competitors leave tax entirely up to you.

<Notice type="info" title="What you keep after fees">
  On the Free plan, a $10 sale nets you roughly: $10 minus 5% Payhip fee ($0.50) minus Stripe's ~2.9% + $0.30 ($0.59) = about $8.91. That ~11% all-in cost is the honest price of zero monthly fees. Factor this in before you price a $5 ebook.
</Notice>

The free plan is the obvious starting point. You pay nothing until you make a sale, and 5% is a very reasonable cut. Once you're doing consistent revenue, here's the math on when to upgrade:

- **Below ~$970/month in sales:** Free plan is cheapest
- **~$970 to ~$3,500/month:** Plus plan ($29/mo at 2%) saves you money
- **Above ~$3,500/month:** Pro plan ($99/mo with 0% fees) becomes the best deal

The breakpoints come from comparing the total cost of each plan at a given revenue level. At $970/month, the free plan's 5% fee ($48.50) just exceeds the Plus plan's $29 + 2% ($48.40). At $3,500/month, Plus costs $99 ($29 + $70) and Pro costs $99, so they're equal. Above that, Pro wins.

Also worth knowing: Payhip lets you process refund requests from your dashboard, but Stripe and PayPal don't return their processing fees on refunds. So a refund on the Free plan costs you the payment processor's fee even though you give the money back.

## When things go wrong

Here are the failure modes you're most likely to hit, and how to fix them.

<Accordion label="Buyer hit the download limit" group="troubleshooting">
By default, customers can only download each file 3 times. If a buyer's internet dropped mid-download or they switched devices, they'll see a "download limit exceeded" message. You can grant additional download credits from your order details page in the dashboard, or raise the default limit in your product settings. If you sell large files, consider bumping the default to 5 attempts.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="Customer didn't get the receipt or download email" group="troubleshooting">
First, ask them to check their spam folder. If it's not there, the most common cause is a typo in their email address at checkout. You can resend the receipt from the order details page in your dashboard. If emails are consistently landing in spam, check that your custom domain's SPF and DKIM records are set up correctly (Payhip's help center has a guide for this). Buyers can also always access their files from the download page link they received.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="Can't connect PayPal or getting Stripe errors" group="troubleshooting">
PayPal connection issues usually mean your PayPal account isn't a business account or your country isn't supported for the integration. For Stripe errors, the most common problem is trying to connect before your Stripe account is fully activated (Stripe may need identity verification or a bank account first). Make sure your Stripe account status is "enabled" in the Stripe dashboard before connecting it to Payhip.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="Missing payment or payout" group="troubleshooting">
Payhip deposits funds to your connected payment account immediately after each transaction. If a sale shows in your Payhip dashboard but the money isn't in your Stripe or PayPal account, check the transaction status in your payment processor's dashboard first. Sometimes funds are held by Stripe or PayPal for their own review, which is outside Payhip's control. For Payhip-specific payout issues, their support can trace individual transactions.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="File won't upload" group="troubleshooting">
The per-file limit is 5GB. If your file is under that and still fails, check your internet connection (large uploads over flaky connections time out). Some browsers block uploads from certain folder locations (like network drives). Try copying the file to your local disk first. Supported formats cover virtually everything, but if you're uploading an executable or archive, some antivirus software on your machine may interfere with the upload.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="A chargeback landed" group="troubleshooting">
Chargebacks happen when a buyer disputes the charge with their bank. Prevention is better than cure: make your product descriptions and previews accurate so buyers know what they're getting, deliver files instantly (Payhip does this), and respond quickly to refund requests before they escalate to disputes. Payhip has a guide on protecting yourself against chargebacks, and you can also block specific countries, states, or individual customers from buying if you see fraud patterns.
</Accordion>
