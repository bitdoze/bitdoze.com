## What hardware OPNsense and pfSense actually need

OPNsense and pfSense run on **FreeBSD**, not Linux. That single fact decides your hardware shopping list before CPU clock speed or RAM ever enters the picture:

1. **Buy Intel NICs (`igc` / `ixl`), skip Realtek (`re`).** Intel's i225-V and i226-V 2.5GbE controllers use FreeBSD's built-in `igc` driver, and Intel's X710 10GbE SFP+ cages (in the MS-01) use `ixl`. Realtek RTL8125 2.5GbE chips have a long history of link drops and watchdog timeouts under FreeBSD unless you install an out-of-tree `realtek-re-kmod` driver package and pray it survives the next point release.
2. **Never use USB Ethernet dongles on a firewall.** USB NICs (`ue0`) re-enumerate slowly at boot, drop packets under sustained NAT table pressure, and break interface assignments if bumped.
3. **Size the CPU to your inspection and VPN workload, not just WAN megabits.** Plain stateful firewalling and NAT take almost no CPU. What eats cores is inline deep-packet inspection (**Suricata IDS/IPS** or **Zenarmor**), PPPoE single-queue framing on certain fiber ISPs, and multi-gigabit VPN tunnels.

## 2 ports vs 4 ports: VLAN trunking vs physical separation

How many Ethernet ports you need depends on how you segment your home network:

- **Two ports (WAN + LAN) with a switch:** `igc0` plugs into your ISP modem/ONT, and `igc1` plugs into a [2.5GbE switch](/best-2-5-10gbe-switch-homelab/). If you want separate Guest, IoT, and Homelab networks on a 2-port box, you create 802.1Q VLANs (`vlan10`, `vlan20`, `vlan30`) on `igc1` and trunk them to a VLAN-aware switch and access point.
- **Four ports (physical interfaces per zone):** `igc0` is WAN, `igc1` is your main LAN switch, `igc2` feeds a dedicated dumb switch or AP for IoT gear, and `igc3` feeds a DMZ or lab server. No 802.1Q tag rules to misconfigure, and each physical port gets its own dedicated 2.5Gbps pipe.

## Bare metal vs running OPNsense in a Proxmox VM

You *can* virtualize OPNsense inside Proxmox VE by passing the physical WAN/LAN NICs (`PCI Passthrough`) or Linux bridges into a VM. I still recommend **running your primary internet firewall on a dedicated bare-metal box**.

When your router is a VM on the same hypervisor you use to test new kernels, storage pools, and containers, every Proxmox host reboot or botched IOMMU change cuts off the internet for the whole house — including your own ability to look up the fix. A dedicated $349–$369 N150 box on a [small UPS](/best-ups-for-home-server/) keeps the Wi-Fi, DNS, and WireGuard access alive while you tear down your lab servers behind it.

## Day-one OPNsense setup & FreeBSD checklist

Four quick adjustments during and right after installation make an OPNsense mini PC significantly more reliable:

### 1. Pick ZFS (not UFS) in the installer

When the OPNsense installer asks for a filesystem, choose **ZFS (Stripe — single disk)** even if your mini PC only has one SSD. ZFS gives you **Boot Environments** (`System > Firmware > Boot Environments`). Before any major OPNsense upgrade, you click one button to snapshot the boot environment; if an update breaks a plugin or driver, you reboot, select the previous snapshot on the HDMI console menu, and you're back online in 30 seconds.

### 2. Verify Intel NIC driver names at the console

At first boot, OPNsense lists detected interfaces:
- **`igc0`, `igc1`, `igc2`, `igc3`**: Intel i225/i226 2.5GbE ports (what you want on EQ14, S13, FW4C, MS-01).
- **`ixl0`, `ixl1`**: Intel X710 10GbE SFP+ cages (on the MS-01).
- **`re0`, `re1`**: Realtek NICs (if you see these on a generic box, install the `os-realtek-re` plugin under `System > Firmware > Plugins` immediately to prevent watchdog stalls).

### 3. Enable PowerD and C-states for low idle heat

Out of the box, FreeBSD often leaves modern Intel CPUs running warmer than Linux does. In the OPNsense web UI:
1. Go to **System > Settings > Miscellaneous > Power Savings** and enable **Use PowerD**, set to `Hiadaptive` on AC power.
2. Under **System > Settings > Tunables**, set `dev.cpu.0.cx_lowest` (and remaining cores) to `C3` so the N150 or i5 enters deep idle states between packet bursts.

### 4. Turn on WireGuard for road-warrior access

Instead of forwarding ports to internal services, configure OPNsense's built-in **VPN > WireGuard** server. Pair it with a [GL.iNet travel router](/best-travel-router-wireguard-tailscale/) in your laptop bag, and every device you bring to a hotel automatically tunnels back through your home firewall.

<Accordion label="What CPU do I need for OPNsense or pfSense in 2026?" group="faq" expanded="true">
Any modern 4-core x86 CPU routes a 1Gbps WAN effortlessly. An Intel N150 (in the Beelink EQ14 and Mini S13) comfortably handles 2.5GbE routing, ~1Gbps WireGuard VPN tunnels, and moderate Suricata or Zenarmor inspection. <Accordion label="Does OPNsense or pfSense require AES-NI?" group="faq">
No. Neither OPNsense nor current pfSense requires AES-NI to install or update. AES-NI accelerates IPsec and OpenVPN, while WireGuard uses ChaCha20-Poly1305 and runs fast on any modern CPU. Every processor recommended in this guide includes AES-NI anyway.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="Are 2 Ethernet ports enough for OPNsense, or do I need 4?" group="faq">
Two Intel 2.5GbE ports (WAN + LAN) are enough for most homes if you connect the LAN port to a switch. If you want isolated Guest, IoT, and Homelab networks on a 2-port box, you use 802.1Q VLANs with a managed switch. A 4-port appliance like the Protectli FW4C lets you plug separate switches or access points into physically isolated ports without configuring VLAN tags.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="Why do people warn against Realtek NICs for OPNsense and pfSense?" group="faq">
OPNsense and pfSense run on FreeBSD, where Intel's `igc` (2.5GbE i225/i226) and `ixl` (10GbE X710) drivers are mature and stable under sustained load. Realtek 2.5GbE chips (`RTL8125`) historically suffered from link drops and watchdog timeouts under FreeBSD's default driver unless you manually maintain the vendor kernel module (`os-realtek-re`).
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="Should I run OPNsense bare metal or inside a Proxmox VM?" group="faq">
Run your primary WAN firewall on bare metal on a dedicated mini PC. Virtualizing OPNsense inside Proxmox works via PCI passthrough, but it means every Proxmox host reboot, kernel update, or storage experiment takes down the entire household's internet and DNS.
</Accordion>
