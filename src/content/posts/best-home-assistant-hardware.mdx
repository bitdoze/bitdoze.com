## Which one do you need?

Three questions decide it:

| Your setup | Right hardware |
| --- | --- |
| Lights, plugs, a few automations, want it done today | Home Assistant Green |
| The above plus add-ons, Frigate, local voice, or you like tinkering | N150 mini PC |
| A drawer Pi and a small setup | Pi 5 with a real SSD |

The HA install docs keep the requirements modest on purpose; the workload that actually decides your hardware is what you bolt on afterward. A bare Home Assistant install sips CPU. Frigate counting cars on four camera feeds and Whisper transcribing voice commands are where 4GB boxes run out of road.

## An N150 mini PC: what I'd buy

The [Beelink EQ14](/go/beelink-eq14/) at $369 (4.5★/105, ships from Amazon) is the practical answer: N150, 16GB RAM, 500GB SSD plus a second M.2 slot for a bigger drive, dual 2.5GbE, internal PSU. Two install routes, both official:

- HAOS bare metal: flash the generic x86-64 image and the box is a bigger, faster Green.
- A Proxmox VM: HAOS as a VM with the Zigbee dongle passed through, leaving the host free for [other containers](/docker-containers-home-server/). This is the route I'd take; the [Proxmox mini PC guide](/best-mini-pc-proxmox/) covers the host setup, and USB passthrough is one dropdown.

The [Mini S13](/go/beelink-mini-s13/) at $349 (4.5★/659) does the identical job for $20 less with a power brick instead of an internal PSU. This is the same class of box that wins the [Plex and Jellyfin](/best-mini-pc-plex-jellyfin/) guide for the same reason: Quick Sync.

## Raspberry Pi 5: only if it's already in a drawer

Home Assistant supports the Pi 4 and 5, and the official install docs let you start from a 32GB microSD card but push you toward a proper power supply and, for anything beyond testing, an SSD. Writes kill cards: Home Assistant's database, logs, and add-on churn wear out microSD in months, and the most common Pi failure story is a corrupted card at the worst time. A Pi 5 with an NVMe HAT is a working Home Assistant box, but by the time you buy the board, case, cooler, PSU, and the SSD adapter you've spent near-N150 money for less compute and more cables. If one is already sitting in a drawer, fine. Otherwise skip it. The Pi AI HAT+ exists for edge inference but the current listing is thin (3.8★ from 11 ratings), so I'm not recommending it.

## Frigate in 2026: what does the detection now

The Coral USB TPU advice in older guides is dead: Frigate's own docs no longer recommend it for new installs and point you at Hailo-8/8L accelerators or an Intel iGPU/NPU through OpenVINO instead ([Frigate docs](https://docs.frigate.video/configuration/object_detectors/)). On an N150 box the iGPU plus OpenVINO is the built-in answer, and it's the second reason these mini PCs beat a Pi or the Green. As a rule of thumb from Frigate's hardware guidance, an N150-class iGPU covers a small multi-camera setup; add cameras and you start wanting a Hailo or more GPU.

The scale ladder is worth knowing before you buy a camera. Each camera stream needs its own detection, so the detector's inference speed multiplies by camera count. The two Hailo options differ by an order of two: the Hailo-8L does 13 TOPS and the full Hailo-8 does 26 TOPS, on M.2 and PCIe cards that Frigate's docs cover. The practical line I draw: one to three cameras, let the N150's iGPU handle it. Four or more, or you want person/car/package detection at higher frame rates, add a Hailo and let OpenVINO rest. Frigate caches its models on first run, so the detector choice happens in the config file, not the BIOS.

## Local voice: Whisper, Piper and Speech-to-Phrase

Local voice has two tiers in practice, and Home Assistant's own voice documentation puts real numbers on the gap. Speech-to-Phrase is the lightweight path: it recognizes a fixed sentence set and responds in under a second even on Green or Pi-class hardware. Full Whisper understands open-ended speech, and the same docs time it at around 8 seconds on a Pi 4 against under a second on an Intel NUC-class box. Piper text-to-speech is cheap on anything.

That 8x gap is the whole hardware argument. If your voice plan is "turn off the kitchen lights" and similar fixed commands, the Green's Speech-to-Phrase tier genuinely works. If you want freeform commands, a voice pipeline that doubles as a home intercom, or Whisper with a model bigger than tiny-int8, you want the N150 box and its 16GB. If voice is a goal at all, buy the N150; the Green's 4GB runs out fast.

## The radio: Zigbee and Thread

For Zigbee2MQTT and ZHA the [SONOFF Zigbee 3.0 Dongle Plus MG24](/go/sonoff-dongle-plus-mg24/) at $35.90 (4.7★/725, ships from Amazon) is the community default: EFR32MG24 radio, 3dBi antenna, works with Zigbee and OpenThread firmware, and it's cheap enough to keep a spare.

Two placement rules from the HA docs: use the bundled USB extension cable, and keep the dongle away from USB 3 ports, which emit enough 2.4GHz noise to bury Zigbee (the ZHA docs call this out specifically). On a Proxmox host, pass the dongle's USB device through to the HAOS VM rather than trusting USB-over-IP tricks. The MG24 is on Zigbee2MQTT's supported-adapters list as well, so the same dongle covers whichever coordinator stack you end up on.

The official alternative is the Home Assistant Connect ZBT-2, on Amazon now at $79 (4.7★/339): MG24 radio plus an ESP32-S3 onboard, an optimized antenna, and four times the baud rate of the old ZBT-1 per Nabu Casa. Note the $49 MSRP, though: the [official page](https://www.home-assistant.io/connect/zbt-2/) lists retailers selling closer to that, so the Amazon price is a premium.

## Starting on Green and moving later

A reasonable worry: buy the $219 Green now, outgrow it, and feel like the money was wasted. It isn't, for two reasons. First, Home Assistant's built-in backup makes the migration boring: Settings > System > Backups, download the full backup, flash HAOS on the new box, and restore it during onboarding. Devices, automations, dashboards, and history come across. Second, the Green resells or redeploys well: it makes a fine dedicated Zigbee/Thread border-router box or a starter hub for someone else's house. The only wasted path is buying the Green, hitting its ceiling in month two, and leaving it in a drawer.

## FAQ

<Accordion label="What hardware do I need for Home Assistant?" group="faq" expanded="true">
Anything from a Green box to an N150 mini PC. The official requirements are modest; the add-ons you bolt on later (Frigate, voice, lots of integrations) are what actually decide how much hardware you want.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="Is the Home Assistant Green worth it at $219?" group="faq">
If you want working Home Assistant tonight and no maintenance, yes. At $219 versus $349 for an N150 mini PC with four times the room to grow, though, the Green is the easy start, not the best value.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="Mini PC or Raspberry Pi 5 for Home Assistant?" group="faq">
Mini PC. A Pi 5 plus PSU, case, cooler, and SSD adapter lands close to N150 money for less compute. Buy the Pi only if you already own one.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="Can the Home Assistant Green run Frigate?" group="faq">
Not realistically. Frigate needs a detector with real throughput, and Green's RK3566 and 4GB RAM aren't it. Use an N150 box with OpenVINO or add a Hailo accelerator.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="Do I need a Zigbee coordinator?" group="faq">
Yes, unless you plan to run only Wi-Fi devices. ZHA and Zigbee2MQTT both need a USB coordinator like the SONOFF MG24; Home Assistant's own ZBT-2 is the official option.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="What are Home Assistant's hardware requirements?" group="faq">
The official docs support x86-64 machines, the Green and Yellow, and Raspberry Pi 4/5. A basic install runs on very little; in practice I want at least 4GB of RAM and an SSD, and 16GB plus an Intel iGPU if Frigate or voice is in the plan.
</Accordion>
