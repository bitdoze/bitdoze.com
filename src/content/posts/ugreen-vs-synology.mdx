## The two ways to think about this

UGREEN and Synology are solving different problems that happen to look the same on a shelf:

- **UGREEN** sells you hardware and lets you figure out the software. UGOS Pro is a competent Debian-based NAS OS with Docker, VMs, and snapshots, though it's a year or two behind DSM in polish. UGREEN's whole pitch is that you get a generation-newer Intel CPU with Quick Sync, 2x to 4x the base RAM, usable NVMe storage pools, and faster networking for less money.
- **Synology** sells you an appliance. DSM is the best consumer NAS operating system ever made, and you pay for it in the form of older CPUs, tiny base RAM on the entry models, no 10GbE on the mid-tier boxes, and drive-compatibility restrictions on the `x25+` series that have burned community goodwill.

## What you actually get with DSM

DSM is the reason people keep paying the Synology tax, and it's not just marketing:

- **Synology Photos** is a genuinely good Google Photos replacement (facial recognition, album sharing, timeline) and it's free forever. UGREEN's Photos app exists but trails it in polish and search quality.
- **Active Backup for Business** backs up Windows PCs, Macs, file servers, and *other* Synology boxes with deduplication. There's no UGREEN equivalent this turnkey.
- **Hyper Backup** handles the off-site copy to S3, Backblaze B2, or another NAS with versioning, client-side encryption, and a restore wizard that actually works.
- **Synology Hybrid RAID (SHR)** lets you mix different drive capacities in a pool without wasting the extra terabytes on larger disks.
- **QuickConnect** gives you zero-config remote access without opening ports. UGOS has remote access too, but QuickConnect is the one that works effortlessly for non-technical relatives.

If you've ever set up a Synology for a parent or a small office, you know why it wins: someone else maintains all of this, and it works for a decade. The [DS223j](/go/synology-ds223j/) at $209.99 (4.5★/890) is the cheapest entry point for that world: ARM CPU, 1GB RAM, 1GbE, direct-play only, but the full DSM storage experience.

## What you get with UGREEN

The NASync DXP boxes are modern mini PCs wearing aluminum NAS enclosures:

- **Real CPUs with Intel Quick Sync.** The Intel N100 in the DXP2800 and the 5-core Pentium Gold 8505 in the DXP4800 Plus are the same silicon family that runs the boxes in our [home server mini PC roundup](/best-mini-pc-home-server/). They do 4K Plex and Jellyfin hardware transcoding (including AV1 decode), Docker stacks, and VMs without breaking a sweat. The Synology DS925+ uses an embedded AMD Ryzen V1500B with *no integrated GPU*, so it cannot hardware-transcode video at all.
- **10GbE without an add-in card.** While Synology finally added 2.5GbE to the DS225+ and DS925+, the DXP4800 Plus includes both **10GbE and 2.5GbE** onboard (and the 6-bay [DXP6800 Pro](/go/ugreen-dxp6800-pro/) ships dual 10GbE plus dual Thunderbolt 4). Meanwhile, the DS925+ dropped the PCIe slot older Synology models used for 10GbE upgrades.
- **NVMe slots that take any SSD.** Two M.2 slots come standard on both the 2-bay and 4-bay UGREEN models, and you can build fast storage pools for Docker containers and Plex databases using any off-the-shelf NVMe stick. The DS225+ has zero M.2 slots, and the DS925+ officially restricts NVMe storage pools to Synology-branded SSDs.
- **First-class Docker, VMs, and third-party OS freedom.** UGOS Pro ships a clean Docker Compose UI and KVM-based VM manager. Even better, the hardware is standard x86 with UGOS on its own internal boot drive, and UGREEN officially allows installing TrueNAS SCALE, Unraid, or Proxmox on an SSD without voiding the hardware warranty.

<Notice type="info" title="The Synology x25+ drive-compatibility catch">
Synology's 2025/2026 `x25+` models (including the DS925+ and DS225+) enforce a verified drive compatibility list for new storage pools. Unverified third-party drives trigger persistent DSM warnings or pool-creation restrictions depending on the model and DSM version. UGREEN doesn't care what SATA or NVMe brand you put in it. If you're buying third-party drives from the [NAS hard drive guide](/best-hard-drives-for-nas/) (IronWolf, Exos, WD Red Plus), check Synology's current compatibility matrix before assuming a DS925+ will accept them cleanly.
</Notice>

## Where UGOS Pro actually falls short

To keep this honest, UGOS Pro is *fine*, not DSM:

- **App ecosystem.** Synology's Package Center has a decade of mature native apps. UGOS's native app center is thin; you'll run Docker containers for anything serious (Immich, Jellyfin, Nextcloud). That's ideal for the audience that self-hosts, less ideal for "my mom's photo backup."
- **No SHR equivalent.** UGOS uses standard Linux mdadm RAID (RAID 0/1/5/6/10), so mixing drive sizes wastes space above the smallest disk in the array.
- **Support track record.** UGREEN is a hardware company that entered the NAS market in 2024; Synology has over a decade of DSM security advisories and long-tail firmware support behind every box.
- **Resale.** Synology boxes hold value on the used market because DSM is the product. UGREEN boxes depreciate like PCs.

## FAQ

<Accordion label="Is UGREEN as reliable as Synology?" group="faq" expanded="true">
The hardware is: Intel N100 / Pentium 8505 / Core i5 processors, standard DDR5 SO-DIMMs, and machined aluminum chassis. The real difference is software longevity. Synology has been shipping DSM updates for well over a decade, while UGOS Pro launched in 2024. The built-in safety net is that UGREEN boxes are unlocked standard x86 machines, so you can install TrueNAS, Unraid, or Proxmox on them forever regardless of what UGREEN's software team does.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="Can UGREEN replace Synology Photos?" group="faq">
Partially. UGOS has a built-in Photos app with local AI recognition and timeline views, and it improves with each release, but it still trails Synology Photos in polish, search accuracy, and family sharing. If turnkey photo backup for non-technical family members is the main job, DSM is still the better answer. Self-hosters who don't mind Docker should run **Immich** on either box; it beats both vendor apps.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="Does Synology really restrict which drives you can use?" group="faq">
On the 2025/2026 `x25+` series (including the DS925+ and DS225+), DSM checks drives against Synology's verified compatibility list when creating pools, and unverified third-party drives trigger warnings or pool restrictions depending on model and DSM version. The J-series (like the DS223j) is more permissive, and UGREEN has no drive brand restrictions at all.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="Which is better for Plex: UGREEN or Synology?" group="faq">
UGREEN, and it isn't close. Both the DXP2800 (Intel N100) and DXP4800 Plus (Pentium Gold 8505) have modern Intel Quick Sync with AV1 decode plus dual NVMe slots to keep the Plex database off spinning disks. On the Synology side, only the 2-bay DS225+ (Celeron J4125) has an Intel iGPU for hardware transcoding; the 4-bay DS925+ uses an AMD Ryzen V1500B with no integrated GPU at all. See our full breakdown in the [best NAS for Plex & Jellyfin guide](/best-nas-for-plex/).
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="What about QNAP or TerraMaster?" group="faq">
TerraMaster's F4-424 Pro ($730.99) is the most direct hardware competitor to the DXP4800 Plus, packing an 8-core i3-N305 and 32GB of DDR5 RAM out of the box; most homelabbers flash TrueNAS or Unraid onto it rather than using TerraMaster's TOS. QNAP offers competitive hardware, but its repeated ransomware incidents and busy QTS interface make UGREEN and Synology the cleaner picks.
</Accordion>
