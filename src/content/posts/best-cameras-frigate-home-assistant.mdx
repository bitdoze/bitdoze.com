## What a camera needs to work well in Frigate

Frigate is pickier than the box specs suggest. Before buying, check for these four things:

1. **RTSP with a usable sub-stream.** Frigate's default recipe runs object detection on the low-resolution sub-stream (cheap on CPU/iGPU) while recording the high-resolution main stream (full detail for clips). A camera that only exposes one stream forces you to detect on the 4K feed, which overwhelms an Intel iGPU (OpenVINO) or Hailo-8 accelerator across multiple cameras (see our [Home Assistant hardware guide](/best-home-assistant-hardware/) for detector sizing). Every Reolink in this list exposes main and sub RTSP endpoints; Amcrest uses `cam/realmonitor?channel=1&subtype=0/1`.
2. **ONVIF.** Not strictly required for Frigate itself, but it's what lets Home Assistant's generic camera/ONVIF integrations pull events, PTZ, and snapshots without the vendor cloud.
3. **PoE, not Wi-Fi.** One cable delivers power *and* data, survives weather, and doesn't clog your 2.4GHz airtime. All picks here are PoE.
4. **No forced cloud.** Everything on this page works fully local: microSD, NVR, or Frigate/HA. Cameras that need a subscription to unlock basic recording (Ring, most of Nest) don't belong in a self-hosted setup.

<Notice type="info" title="4MP/5MP ≠ worse than 4K at night">
A 4MP or 5MP sensor with a larger 1/2.7" or 1/1.8" imager collects more light per pixel than a budget 4K (8MP) sensor with a smaller imager. That's why the 5MP RLC-510A often produces *better* night footage than the 4K RLC-810A in the same scene. Resolution helps identification at distance; sensor size helps everything after dark. For most door and driveway coverage at 3–8 meters, 5MP cameras are the sweet spot.
</Notice>

## H.264 vs H.265, fixed vs varifocal

**Codec:** H.265 (HEVC) halves storage and bandwidth versus H.264, but costs more CPU/iGPU effort to decode for detection and browser scrubbing. The practical answer: record the 4K main stream in H.265 for storage efficiency, and run detection on the H.264 sub-stream so Frigate decodes the cheap codec. All Reolink picks support H.264 on their sub-streams; the 5MP models use H.264 on the main stream too, while the 4K models default to H.265 on the main stream.

**Lens:** fixed lenses are sharper and cheaper for a known scene (a doorway, a driveway). Varifocal models like the RLC-811A let you zoom to frame a specific spot after mounting, which is worth the extra ~$30 only if you genuinely don't know your framing yet, or need a tight shot of a gate 15m away.

## Wiring it into Frigate (the config that works)

A minimal `frigate.yml` using `go2rtc` restreaming to detect on the cheap H.264 sub-stream while recording the full-resolution main stream:

```yaml
go2rtc:
  streams:
    # Use h265Preview_01_main on 4K models (RLC-810A/820A), or h264Preview_01_main on 5MP models (RLC-510A/520A)
    driveway_main: rtsp://admin:password@192.168.30.11:554/h265Preview_01_main
    driveway_sub:  rtsp://admin:password@192.168.30.11:554/h264Preview_01_sub

cameras:
  driveway:
    ffmpeg:
      inputs:
        - path: rtsp://127.0.0.1:8554/driveway_sub
          input_args: preset-rtsp-restream
          roles: [detect]
        - path: rtsp://127.0.0.1:8554/driveway_main
          input_args: preset-rtsp-restream
          roles: [record]
    detect:
      width: 640
      height: 360
      fps: 5
    record:
      enabled: true
      retain:
        days: 7
        mode: motion
    motion:
      mask: 0.0,0.0,0.3,0.0,0.3,1.0,0.0,1.0  # example: ignore a tree on the left third
```

Two Reolink-specific gotchas that bite everyone once:

- **Match the RTSP path to the stream codec (`h265Preview` vs `h264Preview`), and skip `http-flv`.** Reolink's 4K cameras (RLC-810A, 820A, Duo 3) encode their 4K main stream in H.265 by default, which lives at `/h265Preview_01_main` (5MP models like the RLC-510A/520A use `/h264Preview_01_main`). Always keep `/h264Preview_01_sub` on the sub-stream for cheap H.264 detection decode, and avoid Reolink's HTTP-FLV streams, which drop frames and desync under Frigate's continuous load.
- **Update firmware manually, then stop.** Reolink doesn't auto-update from its download center, and major firmware jumps occasionally rename stream paths or toggle RTSP/ONVIF off by default. Enable RTSP and ONVIF in the Reolink client (Network > Advanced > Server Settings), note your working firmware version, and leave it alone unless you need a specific fix.

## Storage math & where the cameras live

Rough numbers for 24/7 recording vs motion retention, per camera per day:

| Stream | Bitrate | Storage/day |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 5MP main (H.264) | ~6 Mbps | ~65 GB |
| 4K main (H.265) | ~8 Mbps | ~85 GB |
| Sub-stream detect (H.264) | ~0.5 Mbps | ~5 GB |

Four 5MP cameras recording 24/7 = ~260–280 GB/day. Frigate's `mode: motion` retention cuts that by ~70% on quiet properties. Size your drive accordingly: the [NAS hard drive guide](/best-hard-drives-for-nas/) covers which CMR disks to put it on, and a [dedicated NAS or DAS](/best-nas-homelab-docker-proxmox/) beats a microSD card for real retention.

On the network side, put cameras on their own **VLAN with no internet access**. They don't need it: Frigate and HA reach *them*, and they never need to phone home. The PoE ports on a [2.5GbE switch with a VLAN-capable setup](/best-2-5-10gbe-switch-homelab/) handle both the power budget and the isolation.

## FAQ

<Accordion label="Do these cameras need a Reolink NVR or cloud subscription?" group="faq" expanded="true">
No. Every camera on this page exposes RTSP and ONVIF directly and can record to microSD or stream to Frigate and Home Assistant. A Reolink NVR is optional convenience; the cloud features are optional and blocked completely when you put the cameras on a no-internet VLAN.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="Why does Frigate want two streams from each camera?" group="faq">
Object detection runs on a low-resolution H.264 sub-stream (e.g. 640×360 at 5fps) because decoding it costs a fraction of the CPU/iGPU of decoding 4K. Recording uses the full-resolution main stream so saved clips have real detail. A single-stream camera forces you to choose between cheap detection and usable recordings.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="Reolink vs Amcrest: which is better for Home Assistant?" group="faq">
Reolink is cheaper per camera, has a larger Home Assistant install base, and exposes clean on-camera AI person/vehicle binary sensors through HA's native Reolink integration, though its firmware occasionally needs manual attention. Amcrest (a Dahua OEM) costs slightly more with more conservative, predictable RTSP/ONVIF firmware. Both work well in Frigate over RTSP.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="Can I run these cameras on Wi-Fi instead of PoE?" group="faq">
Reolink sells Wi-Fi variants, but skip them if you can pull cable. Wi-Fi cameras drop frames under congestion (which causes Frigate decode errors and false motion glitches), clog 2.4GHz airtime, and still need a power cable run to an outlet anyway. If you have to drill a hole for power, run a single Cat6 cable for PoE instead.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="How much storage do I need for four cameras?" group="faq">
Four 5MP cameras at ~65GB/day each use ~260–280GB/day for continuous 24/7 recording, or roughly 80–100GB/day with motion-only retention. A single 8TB CMR NAS drive holds 3–4 weeks of continuous footage; a 12TB drive is comfortable.
</Accordion>
