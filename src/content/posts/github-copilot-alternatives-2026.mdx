## What changed with GitHub Copilot usage-based pricing

The old system used premium request units (PRUs). You got a monthly allotment, each model had a fixed cost per request, and when you ran out, Copilot fell back to a cheaper model. That system is gone. What replaced it is token-based billing through GitHub AI Credits, and the details matter more than the headline.

### What stays the same

- Base plan prices have not changed: Pro is $10/month, Pro+ is $39/month, Business is $19/user/month, Enterprise is $39/user/month.
- Code completions and Next Edit suggestions remain included and unlimited on all paid plans. They do not consume AI Credits. (On Copilot Free, completions are capped at 2,000 per month.)
- Model selection is still available. The picker now includes 25+ models across OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Microsoft, xAI, and Moonshot.

For full feature coverage beyond billing, see the [GitHub Copilot Pro guide](/github-copilot-complete-guide/).

*As of November 2026, per GitHub's official plans documentation.*

### What is different now

The biggest behavioral change: **no more fallback**. Under the old PRU system, exhausting your units dropped you to a cheaper model and kept working. The April 27 announcement was explicit: "Fallback experiences will no longer be available." When credits run out now, you are done until you take action.

<Notice type="warning" title="No more fallback">
If you relied on the old fallback-to-cheaper-model behavior when PRUs ran out, that safety net is gone. Budget accordingly.
</Notice>

A quick PRU explainer for anyone who remembers the old system: PRUs (the "Copilot premium requests" of 2025) were a fixed cost-per-request currency. The new AI Credits are a fixed cost-per-token currency. Same idea, different meter.

Other differences worth knowing:

- Usage varies wildly by model. A completion is free. A chat question costs pennies. A multi-hour agentic session on a frontier model can eat a large chunk of your monthly allotment.
- Copilot code review also consumes GitHub Actions minutes on top of AI Credits. More on that in the cost section.
- Annual plan holders stay on legacy PRU billing until their plan expires, with model multipliers that increased on June 1. At expiration you drop to Copilot Free, with three options: upgrade to a monthly plan, convert to monthly early with prorated credits, or cancel with a prorated refund.

### Plans, flex allotments, and the Max tier

On May 12, 2026, GitHub introduced flex allotments and a new Copilot Max plan. The original "credits equal your plan price" model was revised. Here is the current layout (per GitHub docs and the May 12 announcement by Joe Binder, accessed November 2026):

| Plan | Price/mo | Base credits | Flex | Total AI credits | Token value |
|------|----------|--------------|------|------------------|-------------|
| Copilot Free | $0 | "an allowance" | n/a | limited (2,000 completions/mo; auto model selection only) | n/a |
| Copilot Pro | $10 | 1,000 | 500 | **1,500** | $15 |
| Copilot Pro+ | $39 | 3,900 | 3,100 | **7,000** | $70 |
| Copilot Max | $100 | 10,000 | 10,000 | **20,000** | $200 |
| Copilot Business | $19/seat | 1,900/user | n/a | pooled at billing entity | $19/user |
| Copilot Enterprise | $39/seat | 3,900/user | n/a | pooled at billing entity | $39/user |
| Copilot access (enterprise) | $0 (GHE Cloud) / $10 Standalone | none | n/a | metered from first use | n/a |

**1 AI credit = $0.01 USD.** That is the conversion to remember.

Base credits match the plan price 1:1 and never change. Flex allotments are extra credits on top; they can vary over time as model economics change. So Pro gives you 1,500 credits ($15 of token value) for $10, not just $10 in credits. That is better than the original announcement, but the totals still matter when you do the math.

**Copilot Max** is new (May 12, 2026): $100/month for 20,000 credits ($200 of token value). It targets heavy agentic users who would otherwise burn through Pro+ in a week.

The **Copilot access** tier (enterprise, available via account teams since October 28, 2026) is interesting for orgs with many light users: $0 per seat for GHE Cloud-licensed users or $10/user/month Standalone, with no included allowance and pure metered usage. Dangerous for heavy agentic users, fine for the person who only wants completions.

### When credits run out: overage and budgets

The original article said "when credits run out, you are done." That was too absolute. Three options exist now (per GitHub docs, accessed November 2026):

1. Upgrade your plan. Pay only the price difference. Earlier-month usage counts toward the new allowance immediately (proration).
2. Set an additional-usage budget. Keep working. Overage is billed at $0.01/credit after the fact, separately from your subscription renewal. Additional usage may be capped based on your usage patterns, billing history, and verification status. To continue past a cap, you must pay off the overage already consumed.
3. Wait for the monthly reset. Included credits do not carry over. The allowance resets at 00:00:00 UTC on the 1st of each calendar month, not on your billing date.

<Notice type="warning" title="Overage billing footgun">
Additional usage is billed after it occurs, separately from your subscription renewal. You might receive a charge after you cancel, since final-days usage may not appear on the last invoice. Set a $0 hard-stop budget if you want a firm ceiling (see the checklist below).
</Notice>

For a hard cap, GitHub lets you set a "Bundled AI credits budget" to $0 USD and enable "Stop usage when budget limit is reached" under GitHub -> Settings -> Billing -> budgets. This is the guaranteed spend ceiling.

On Business and Enterprise, credits are pooled across the billing entity. Users who exhaust the pool see a banner to request more budget from their administrator (budget-increase requests went GA on September 16, 2026).

One footgun on mobile: iOS/Android in-app subscriptions cannot buy additional credits through the app store. Move billing to GitHub.com if you need overage.

### The June-August promo (ended)

<Notice type="info" title="Historical note">
The transition promo has ended. Business got $30/month in credits and Enterprise got $70/month (up from $19/$39) during June through August 2026. Standard allowances are now the ones in the table above: 1,900 and 3,900 credits per user, pooled.
</Notice>

## How much does Copilot cost in AI credits?

This is the part the announcement glossed over. Published API rates determine how fast you burn credits. The earlier version of the table used retired models (GPT-4o-mini, Claude Opus 4.6). Here is the same math with November 2026 models and costs expressed in AI credits (1 credit = $0.01):

| Task | Model | Token usage (approx) | Credit cost |
|------|-------|---------------------|-------------|
| Code completion | any | ~500 tokens | free (not billed) |
| Chat question | Claude Sonnet 4.6 | 2K in / 500 out | ~1-2 credits (~$0.014) |
| Multi-file refactor | Claude Opus 5.5 | 20K in / 5K out | ~18 credits (~$0.18) |
| 1h agentic session | Claude Opus 5.5 | 200K in / 50K out | ~180 credits (~$1.80) uncached |
| 4h frontier session | Claude Fable 5.2 | 1M in / 250K out | ~1,800-3,000 credits ($18-30) |

The headline number: **one 4-hour agentic session on a frontier model exceeds Pro's entire 1,500-credit monthly allotment.** If you do that twice a month, you are on Pro+ or buying overage.

<Notice type="info" title="Cached tokens change the math">
Cached input is roughly 10x cheaper on most models (Sonnet 5.5 cached input: $0.10/M vs $2.00/M uncached). Long sessions that reuse context cost much less than the worst-case numbers above. Most models also add a cache-write cost. Keep your main conversation on one model to preserve the cache.
</Notice>

### Copilot code review costs

Copilot code review bills AI Credits and GitHub Actions minutes per review. The model is auto-selected and not disclosed, so per-token cost varies. GitHub publishes effort-level estimates:

| Effort level | Cost range per review | Average |
|-------------|----------------------|---------|
| Lite | $0.05-$1 | ~$0.25 |
| Balanced | $0.25-$5 | ~$1.50 |
| Max | $1.50-$25 | ~$10 |
| Adaptive | picks within a configured maximum | varies |

Minutes attribute to the repo (and to your enterprise/cost center). AI credits attribute to the member or org, depending on the "Choose how members with a Copilot license are billed" policy. Bot-authored PRs bill to the org.

Ops tip: org admins can set a default Actions runner for code review. Use cheaper or self-hosted runners to cut minute costs. Inspect usage via GitHub Actions metrics filtered to the `copilot-pull-request-reviewer` workflow or the billing usage report (`dynamic/agents/copilot-pull-request-reviewer`).

## 5 cheaper GitHub Copilot alternatives

If Copilot's credit math does not work for your usage pattern, these five options give you more control over spend. I have ordered them by how much I would recommend them.

### 1. OpenCode (best overall alternative)

OpenCode is the open-source terminal coding agent with 228K+ GitHub stars (repo: `anomalyco/opencode`, very active development, v2.3.2 on November 18, 2026). You pick the model and the provider. No subscription. You pay for API tokens at cost.

<Button text="OpenCode Setup Guide" link="/opencode-setup-guide/" variant="solid" color="blue" size="md" icon="arrow-right" />

A concrete cost example with [MiniMax](https://go.bitdoze.com/minimax) M2.7 at $0.25/M input, $1.00/M output: a typical coding session (50K input, 10K output) costs about $0.02. Run 100 sessions a month for $2. If you want more context, MiniMax M3 runs $0.30/M input, $1.20/M output with a 1M window.

Model choice is the killer feature. Use MiniMax M3 for routine edits, GLM-5.3 for complex refactors, DeepSeek V4 Pro for big repos. DeepSeek V4 Flash at $0.098/M input is the cheapest option if you really want to squeeze costs. The model roster churns, so check the [cheapest AI models under $1/M tokens](/best-cheap-models-hermes-agent/) for current pricing.

Against Copilot on cost: no subscription floor, no credits to burn through. You switch models per task with a keystroke instead of picking from a metered picker.

### 2. OpenAI Codex CLI (free with a ChatGPT plan)

Codex CLI is OpenAI's open-source terminal agent (136K GitHub stars, releases shipping weekly). If you already pay for ChatGPT Plus ($20/month), Pro ($200/month), Business, Edu, or Enterprise, Codex comes with the plan at no extra API cost for the default model.

The model lineup has shifted. GPT-5.4 was retired from Codex on August 31, 2026. GPT-5.5 was retired on October 14, 2026. OpenAI now recommends GPT-6.2 Astra for demanding work and GPT-6.2 Sol for everyday coding. GPT-6.1 Sol is the documented replacement if you are moving off GPT-5.5 on a paid plan. GPT-6.1 Terra also works (CLI >= 0.159.0):

```bash
codex --model gpt-6.2-sol
codex exec -m gpt-6.2-astra "Review the current changes"
# in an interactive session: /model
```

In `config.toml`:
```toml
model = "gpt-6.2-sol"
```

If you are already paying for ChatGPT, Codex CLI is a free add-on with strong agentic capabilities. It supports MCP servers and ships with a desktop app (`codex app`), a web agent, and IDE extensions for VS Code, Cursor, and Windsurf.

The limitation is nuanced. There is no first-class Anthropic or Google model picker. But Codex now supports custom model providers via a compatible Responses API endpoint, so "you can only use OpenAI models" is no longer strictly true. See the [Codex app with any model](/codex-app-any-model/) for how to route through other providers. API-key sessions are not bound by ChatGPT-plan model retirements.

### 3. Aider (lightweight and provider-agnostic)

Aider is a minimal terminal coding agent that works with any LLM provider. It has the strongest git integration of anything on this list and handles obscure providers well.

You pay for API tokens at cost, the same pricing as using the models directly.

Aider is the most provider-agnostic option here. If you need to connect to a niche provider or a local Ollama instance, Aider handles it. It supports images and web pages in chat, has architect/discuss modes, and its git workflow (auto-commits, dirty commits, repo maps) is the most complete of the group.

<Notice type="warning" title="Maintenance status">
Aider's last release was v0.86.2 on February 12, 2026. It still works, but the project is dormant compared to OpenCode and Codex CLI, which ship weekly. The README's recommended-models line ("Claude 3.7 Sonnet, DeepSeek R1, GPT-4o") is itself a stale-model tell. Factor dormancy into your choice. The alternatives graveyard is real: [what happened to Amp Code's free tier](/amp-code-free-ai-coding-agent/) is a good reminder that tools can stall or pivot.
</Notice>

The limitation: there is no formal plan mode (architect mode is the closest). The dormant release cadence means bugs and provider API changes may take longer to get fixed.

### 4. OpenCode Go (flat-rate model bundle)

If you want one subscription that covers many models, [OpenCode Go](/opencode-go-plan/) is the strongest flat-rate bundle right now. The $10/month tier includes 31 models (GPT 6 Luna, Claude Haiku 5.5, Grok 4.7, Kimi K3, [GLM-5.3/5.3-Flash](https://go.bitdoze.com/glm) from Zhipu, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, MiniMax M2.7/M3, Qwen3.8 Max/Flash, and more). ### 5. Local models with Ollama (zero API cost)

If you have decent hardware (16GB+ RAM or a GPU), you can run coding models locally through Ollama and point OpenCode or Aider at them.

Per-token cost is zero. You pay for electricity and hardware only.

You get complete privacy, no API keys, no subscriptions, and no rate limits. Good for air-gapped or sensitive-code work.

The limitation: local models are weaker than API models for complex coding tasks. A 32B model handles simple edits and refactoring, but for multi-file changes across a large codebase, you want an API model. The quality gap is real but narrowing. ### Hard-cap budget setup

This is the "guaranteed spend ceiling" equivalent. Concrete steps:

1. Go to GitHub -> Settings -> Billing -> budgets.
2. Set "Bundled AI credits budget" to **$0 USD**.
3. Enable **"Stop usage when budget limit is reached"**.

That is it. When credits are exhausted, usage stops and you get no surprise overage charges.

Failure modes to watch for:

- Client version floor: VS Code >= 1.120, Visual Studio 2022 >= 17.14.33, VS 2026 >= 18.6.0, SSMS >= 22.6, JetBrains >= 1.9.1, Eclipse >= 0.18.0, Xcode >= 0.50.0, Copilot CLI >= 1.0.48. Older clients show wrong pricing and usage. Update before you trust the numbers.
- Mobile in-app subscriptions cannot buy additional credits. Move billing to GitHub.com.
- Overage is billed separately from renewal. Surprise charges are possible even after cancellation.

## The bigger picture

Every AI provider is heading toward usage-based pricing. Anthropic, Google, and OpenAI have all adjusted pricing in the last year. The flat-rate era for AI coding tools is ending because agentic usage (long sessions, many tool calls, large contexts) costs far more to serve than simple completions. This is not a GitHub-specific cash grab; it is the economics catching up.

Six months in, the honest update on "Copilot does not give you that control" is that Copilot now has real cost-control levers: BYOK (bring your own key works even without a subscription), auto model selection (10% discount), Copilot Fusion (task-aware orchestration with a 30% discount through November 30, 2026), session and credit limits, and user-level budgets. The alternatives no longer win on "control" as cleanly as they did in April.

Where alternatives still win is **economics and vendor lock**. OpenCode with your own API key means paying token prices with zero subscription. Switching between MiniMax, DeepSeek, GLM, and Anthropic models is a keystroke, not a plan change. Copilot makes you pick from its metered menu. That gap is structural.

The convergence is real, though. Third-party coding agents (Claude Code, Codex as agent providers) can now bill through Copilot credits, blurring the "Copilot vs alternatives" line. If you want one entry point across multiple agents, a routing layer like [Agent Router](https://go.bitdoze.com/agentrouter) simplifies it.

The developers who will be fine are the ones who use the right model for the right task. A $0.25/M model for routine edits. A $2/M model for complex refactors. A $4/M model only when you need maximum accuracy. GitHub's own cost-optimization guidance now echoes this philosophy. OpenCode makes it easy because you switch models with a keystroke.
