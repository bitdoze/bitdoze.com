## Count the ports, then the power

Write the devices down before you add a switch to the cart. A typical small lab is: router, NAS, Proxmox host, desktop, access point, printer. That is six, and the SG108's eight ports leave two spares. Add a second server, a NVR, and another AP and you are shopping for sixteen ports whether you wanted to or not. Buy two spare ports on purpose. The Sunday you need one is the Sunday the 5-port was the wrong economy.

Then mark which of those devices need Power over Ethernet. An access point or a PoE camera plugged into the SG108 still needs an injector or a PoE switch. The default pick on this page does not power anything. If two or more devices want PoE, skip ahead to that section and buy that switch instead of "the 2.5GbE switch plus a handful of injectors."

The wattage of the switch itself is not the decision. TP-Link's spec for the SG108S-M2 is 9.11W max, from a 12V 1A adapter, in a 6.2 by 4.0 by 1.0 inch metal case. ServeTheHome measured 1.6W idle and 2.0W with one 2.5GbE link up ([their review](https://www.servethehome.com/tp-link-tl-sg108s-m2-8-port-2-5gbe-switch-review/2/)). Even at the 9.11W ceiling, a month at $0.16 per kWh is about a dollar. Fanless also means silent. Pick the switch for ports and for whether you trust the brand, not for the power bill.

## What 2.5GbE is for

A 1GbE link tops out near 110 MB/s. A single modern hard drive can do that. Two drives in a mirror, or one NVMe, cannot. 2.5GbE is about 280 MB/s, which is enough for a 4-bay NAS of spinning disks and for a [mini PC](/best-mini-pc-home-server/) that is not moving VM images all day. 10GbE is about 1.1 GB/s, which is the number you want when Proxmox storage, backups, or a photo library live on the NAS and you are the impatient person waiting.

You do not need a 10GbE port on every run to a laptop. You need it on the two machines that talk to each other the most. That is why the interesting cheap switches this year are not "all ports 10GBase-T." They are 2.5GbE desks with one or two SFP+ cages.

TP-Link's own bullet for the SG108S-M2 says you can stay on existing Cat5e for 2.5GbE. That matches how these PHYs were designed: 2.5GBASE-T was built to run on Cat5e at the usual 100 meter channel, and in practice short in-house Cat5e is fine. 10GBase-T is the picky one. Use Cat6 for short 10GbE copper runs and Cat6a if the cable is long or bundled with a dozen others. If both ends are SFP+, skip copper. A DAC for anything in the same rack, fiber if the run leaves the rack. 10GBase-T modules run hot, draw more power, and many cheap switches are grumpy if you fill every cage with one.

## 2. UGREEN: the $50 switch with one 10GbE port

**$49.99** · 4.5 stars from 307 ratings · 5x 2.5GbE + 1x 10G SFP+

![UGREEN 5x 2.5GbE plus 10G SFP+ switch](https://m.media-amazon.com/images/I/614BWtZs-tL._AC_SL1500_.jpg)

This is the switch I would add when the NAS or the Proxmox box has an SFP+ port and everything else is 2.5GbE. The SFP+ cage takes a 1G, 2.5G, or 10G module, optical or RJ45. The module is not in the box. Budget a DAC or a transceiver, or the 10GbE port is a hole.

The front button is the part people misunderstand. UGREEN documents three modes:

- Standard. A normal switch.
- Link aggregation. Ports 4 and 5 become a group, which they rate at 5Gbps combined. Useful if the NAS has two 2.5GbE ports and supports LACP. Both ends have to be configured. The button alone does not teach your NAS anything. A single file copy still rides one link. The second gigabit shows up when several clients talk at once, not when you drag one folder.
- VLAN. Ports 1 through 4 cannot talk to each other. They can talk to ports 5 and 6. That is port isolation, the pattern people use so guests cannot see each other but can see the router. It is not an 802.1Q VLAN database. You cannot trunk VLAN 10 and VLAN 20 through this box and have them come out tagged on a hypervisor.

Link lights: green means 2.5GbE, yellow means some other speed. If you paid for 2.5GbE and the light is yellow, stop and check the cable and the NIC before you blame the switch.

An unmanaged 2.5GbE switch does not power an access point. Three ways out, in the order I would try them.

**One AP.** Buy a PoE injector and keep the SG108. An injector is cheaper and dumber than replacing the switch, and the data path stays on the switch you already trust.

**A few APs or cameras, and you want a known brand.** The [TP-Link TL-SG105PP-M2](/go/tp-link-tl-sg105pp-m2/) was $125 (list $169.99) with 4.5 stars from 77 ratings. Only two were left, and the seller was Technology Traders rather than Amazon. Read the return line. Five ports also means this is a PoE edge switch, not the core of a growing lab.

**You already run UniFi, or you want a real controller.** The [Ubiquiti Flex 2.5G PoE](/go/ubiquiti-flex-2-5g-poe/) (USW-Flex-2.5G-8-PoE) was $199, 4.6 stars from 134 ratings. Eight 2.5GbE PoE++ ports and a 10GbE RJ45/SFP+ combo uplink. The PoE budget depends on how you feed it: Ubiquiti's listing says 196W with the optional AC adapter, and only 16W, 46W, or 76W if you power the switch itself from upstream PoE. ## The cable that makes the SFP+ port real

A cage without a module is a hole. In the same rack, buy a passive DAC, not a pair of RJ45 transceivers. The [10Gtek 1m SFP+ DAC](/go/10gtek-sfp-dac-1m/) was $14.99, 4.7 stars from 314 ratings, and it is the one I would put between a Proxmox host and this switch. One meter is the right length when the machines are on the same shelf. Passive copper gets unhappy as it gets long. If the NAS is in another room, use fiber and a pair of modules, and accept that those modules cost more than the DAC and run warmer.

10GBase-T modules in particular run hot. A switch that is fine with two DACs can throttle or error when every cage has a copper module in it. Do not fill a cheap SFP+ switch with RJ45 modules and then blame the NAS.

## Prove the speed

Install iperf3 on both ends. On the NAS or the server:

```bash
iperf3 -s
```

On the other machine:

```bash
iperf3 -c 192.168.1.10 -t 20
iperf3 -c 192.168.1.10 -t 20 -R
```

Read the Gbit/s line, not the feelings. Roughly:

| Link you think you bought | iperf3 result that means you got it |
| --- | --- |
| 1GbE | 0.94 Gbit/s |
| 2.5GbE | 2.3 to 2.35 Gbit/s |
| 10GbE | 9.3 to 9.4 Gbit/s |

If the number is a clean step below (2.5GbE gear showing 0.94), something in the path negotiated down. Walk the LEDs. On the UGREEN, yellow is the tell. On a NIC, `ethtool` shows the actual speed. Bad crimps, a leftover 1GbE switch, and a USB Ethernet dongle from 2016 are the usual villains. Cat5e is rarely the villain at 2.5GbE across a house.

Run it for twenty seconds at least. A two-second test fits in a buffer and lies.

Jumbo frames are optional and usually a waste of an evening. Every NIC, the switch, and the NAS have to agree. Leave the MTU at 1500 until a specific protocol's documentation tells you otherwise. The UGREEN's "12K jumbo frames" bullet is a capability, not a recommendation.

## Where the VLANs live

An unmanaged switch forwards frames. Most of them forward VLAN-tagged frames without understanding them, which means you can trunk from an OPNsense box to a Proxmox bridge through a dumb switch and have it work. Test it. A few cheap switches strip tags.

What a dumb switch cannot do is terminate those VLANs onto different physical ports with a policy. The UGREEN's VLAN mode is a fixed isolation trick, useful for "these four ports should not see each other." Real per-VLAN rules belong on the router. If you want a switch UI, buy a managed switch on purpose and do not expect the SG108 to grow one in a firmware update. It will not.
