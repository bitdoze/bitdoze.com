## What the screen recorder for Mac does now

The trick behind the app has not changed. It records the screen without the cursor, stores the mouse movement separately and draws the cursor back in afterwards. That is why you can edit zoom, cursor size and motion after recording instead of locking them in while you record.

### Zoom and cursor

- **Automatic zoom** follows clicks and keeps the zoomed area positioned so the camera bubble does not cover the cursor. Screen Studio creates zooms by default and shows them as purple blocks on the zoom timeline, where you add, drag, disable or remove them by hand. The switch for the automatic ones is Create zooms automatically under Settings > Recording. Automatic zooms come from clicks, so for a stretch with no clicks add a manual zoom.
- **Smooth cursor** turns a shaky pointer path into a glide with motion blur. Cursor size and style are editable after recording, and smoothing can be switched off for a single segment, which the guide suggests for drop-down menus.
- **Glass Loupe** (new in Screen Studio 4) magnifies a small detail under a lens while the rest of the screen stays visible. An upscaler sharpens zoomed areas with a model the vendor trained on macOS screenshots, and it runs on your Mac.
- **Click effects** draw a circle or ripple on each click, and an optional click sound sits in the Cursor tab with its own volume.

### Camera and sound

- **Camera layouts** are fullscreen camera, split screen, camera overlay, screen only and Camera cutout (beta). Cutout removes your background on the Mac and needs Apple silicon with macOS 15 or later. A layouts timeline switches layouts mid video with an animated transition.
- **Webcam polish** arrived in version 4. It adds face tracking, hiding the camera while you are silent, a camera crop and a sharpen option. Color grading offers 12 preset looks or your own LUT file.
- **Microphone**: system audio, the mic or both. The Microphone audio mode picker (called Improve microphone audio before version 4) offers Standard, Noise reduction, Podcast (beta), Loud and Off. Noise reduction and Podcast need Apple silicon and macOS 14 or later; an Intel Mac gets Standard, Loud and Off. Since 4.1 a lip sync setting measures the camera lag and lines the picture up with your voice.
- **Edit by transcript**: delete a sentence in the transcript and Screen Studio cuts that segment from the video. It can also shorten long pauses and filler words, though filler words need a cloud transcript.

### Captions

Captions used to be a single auto-generated track. There are now three routes. The Whisper model runs locally (Base, Small or Medium). Apple Speech Recognition (macOS 26 or later) also runs locally. Cloud transcription uploads your microphone audio and needs macOS 26 and a Screen Studio account. On macOS 26 the vendor removes Whisper from the Transcript method, which leaves Apple Speech as the local option there. Captions are off by default and need a recorded mic track. You can style the font, color, position and word-by-word animation, edit the transcript and export it as an SRT that follows your cuts. If you dictate rather than type, pair the app with a dictation tool such as [FluidVoice](/fluidvoice-mac-dictation/). For other speech-to-text engines, see these [Whisper alternatives](/openai-whisper-alternatives/), and for a transcript of a finished YouTube video, see [YouTube to SRT with AssemblyAI](/transcribe-youtube-video-srt-assemblyai/).

### Backgrounds, device frames and shortcuts

Wallpapers, images, colors and gradients are still there, plus Unsplash search (needs a Screen Studio account). You can save padding and shadow presets and share them with a team. You can record an iPhone or iPad over USB and get a device frame around it. Keystrokes can appear as real keycaps. Masks hide sensitive data by blending with what they cover, but a mask does not follow the page when it scrolls. Version 4.1 added an automatic Hide sensitive data switch for emails, phone numbers, card numbers and keys, plus your own phrases. Background music comes from a built-in royalty-free library or your own MP3 and MP4 files. To put a plain screenshot in a device frame instead, see [Shots.so](/shots-so-mockups/).

## Recording modes: what each one captures

The recording modal has four sources, and two more routes start elsewhere. The choice decides what the editor can do afterwards, because automatic zooms need mouse clicks that only a Mac recording provides.

| Mode | What it captures | Use it when |
|---|---|---|
| Display | Everything on one display; move the mouse to it and click | An app runs in full-screen mode (the guide says Display, not Window, for that) or you move between several windows |
| Window | One window, picked by a click or from the list behind a right-click on the Window button | A single app demo where the desktop should stay out |
| Area | A region you drag or size with exact numbers | A fixed player size, or only part of a page |
| Device | An iPhone or iPad over USB, with a device frame; the Mac's mic and webcam can join | A mobile app demo, expecting no automatic zooms and no cursor |
| iPhone Mirroring | The iPhone Mirroring app window, recorded as a window | The iPhone is already paired with macOS 15 or later |
| Existing video | An MP4 or MOV from another recorder | It only needs zooms, backgrounds and captions |

At first launch macOS asks for four permissions: Accessibility, camera, microphone and screen recording. Grant them in System Settings. Without the screen recording permission there is nothing to record. Open the recording modal with option+cmd+enter or the menu bar icon and start with the Record button. There are four ways to stop. Click Stop Recording on the control widget or on the menu bar icon (the notch hides that icon on some MacBooks). Or right-click the Dock icon and pick Finish Recording, or use the global shortcut you set during onboarding. The widget never lands in the video on macOS 12.3 or later, and it also pauses, restarts and deletes the take.

Speaker notes appear in a prompter that stays out of the recording. Press ⌘⌥⌃F to flag a moment on the timeline that deserves a cut. Notifications stay out of recordings unless you turn on Allow notifications when mirroring or sharing display. Screen Studio writes the take to disk every two seconds, so a crash should not cost the recording.

## Pricing model

Screen Studio pricing is no longer a one-time purchase. New customers pick a yearly or monthly personal plan, every feature sits in every plan, and students and educators get a discount. There is a separate Teams page for companies. Check the current numbers on the [Screen Studio pricing page](https://screen.studio/pricing).

If you bought a one-time license back when I wrote the original review, the official FAQ says that license stays valid. You can renew it if your access to new updates has lapsed. One-time licenses are no longer sold to new buyers. Activation works with a code sent to your email, and older one-time licenses activate from the bottom of the same window.

The app is not free, and the FAQ answers that question with a plain no. The download page adds that without an active plan you can use every feature except exporting video files. You can record and edit before you pay, but you cannot walk away with an MP4. Where that wall sits inside the app is untested here.

## Export settings explained

Export offers the format, a frame rate, an output size and a quality level, either to a file or straight to the clipboard. The vendor's own export guide gives useful rules, collected here with the 4.1 additions.

| Setting | Options | What the vendor says |
|---|---|---|
| Format | MP4, GIF, or ProRes since 4.1 | MP4 for anything long; GIF export is slow and not recommended past one minute; ProRes for another editor |
| Output size | 720p, 1080p or 4K for MP4; GIF up to 1080p | 4K takes about four times longer than HD, so ask whether the platform even shows it |
| Frame rate | Up to 60 fps | 24 or 30 fps is the standard for online video and exports faster and smaller; 60 suits high-motion content |
| Quality | A quality level; GIF uses balanced by default with a High quality switch | Compression does not change export time |
| Destination | Export to File or Copy to Clipboard | Since 4.1 the export runs while you choose the folder, and ⓘ in the progress window shows the output path |

On export speed, my 2023 observation was that a long clip took roughly three times its length to export on an M1 MacBook Air. Version 4 ships a new rendering engine, and the vendor claims exports up to three times faster on Apple silicon. Version 4.1 adds its own H.264 encoder for MP4 and a GIF encoder that writes files about a third of the size. I have not measured any of that on current hardware, so treat the old number as a worst case from the first release.

There is also a shareable link that streams the video in a browser instead of sending a file, at 1080p and 60 fps, with comments tied to a timestamp. Shareable links cap recordings at 30 minutes, so longer projects go out as a file. Projects themselves are .screenstudio files. ⌘S writes them to the default projects folder, which you can change under Settings > General, and File > Show previous projects opens it. Zip a project before you send it to someone, because some cloud services treat it as a folder.

Version 4 opens version 3 projects and presets, though the vendor says zooms in those older projects may frame a different part of the recording. Projects saved in version 4 do not open in version 3.

## Pros and cons

### Pros

- Automatic zoom and smooth cursor with almost no editing skill needed.
- Camera cutout, split screen and transcript editing cover most demo and course workflows.
- Works offline, with captions and voice cleanup processed on the Mac.
- One recording reframes to 16:9, 9:16, 1:1, 4:3 or 3:4 output.
- Version 4.1 hides sensitive data on the Mac and exports ProRes for a real editor.

### Cons

- Subscription only for new buyers, no one-time option.
- macOS only, and several version 4 features need Apple silicon and a recent macOS.
- Cloud transcription and shareable links upload audio or video, so read the prompts if the content is sensitive.
- GIF export is slow on anything but short clips.
- The guide lags the app: it still lists options that version 4 removed, such as Always keep zoomed in and the advanced motion blur settings.

## Should you buy it?

Buy it if you regularly ship product demos, courses or tutorial Shorts from a Mac and editing, not recording, is what eats your time. If you make one video a quarter, the subscription stings, and the built-in Screenshot toolbar or QuickTime Player plus a free editor is the cheaper path.

It is for developers, DevOps engineers and course makers who produce walkthroughs often and do not want a video editor in the loop. Typical uses are product demos, release notes as video and bug reports with the cursor already following the click. Skip it if you need Windows or Linux. Skip it if your only Mac is an Intel machine on macOS 13, which gets no voice cleanup, no cutout and no Apple Speech captions. Skip it if a raw capture that you finish in a real editor is all you need, since QuickTime Player does that for free. It also does not replace an editor yet. The vendor's roadmap lists text slides and annotations as planned and multi-clip projects as in progress.

For still screenshots rather than video, [Shottr](/shottr-mac-screenshot-tool/) covers annotation and scrolling captures for free. If you want an AI coding agent to build scripted Shorts and animated explainers across macOS, Windows and Linux instead of recording your screen, read our [HyperFrames Studio review](/hyperframes-studio-review/). To script intros instead of recording them, see the [Python video intro editor](/python-video-intro-editor/). To cut a long recording into captioned 9:16 clips on your own server, see [PeakCut on Docker](/peakcut-self-hosted-ai-video-clipper/).

The app is developed by [Adam Pietrasiak](https://twitter.com/pie6k), who is still the name on the official site's copyright line.

Yes. Click the system audio button in the recording modal and pick all apps or a group of them, and Screen Studio ignores the rest. The microphone and webcam are separate switches, and you need a mic track for captions or transcript editing. At first launch macOS asks for Accessibility, camera, microphone and screen recording permissions.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="Can Screen Studio make 9:16 videos for Shorts and Reels?" group="faq">
Yes. The aspect ratio picker above the preview offers Auto, Wide 16:9, Vertical 9:16, Square 1:1, Classic 4:3 and Tall 3:4, and you can export one recording in several of them. Zooms re-aim and captions move for the new shape, and Reel mode overlays the Instagram or TikTok interface so you can check that nothing important hides under it. Export the MP4 at up to 4K and 60 fps.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="How long does a Screen Studio export take?" group="faq">
Longer than the clip. The vendor's guide says 4K takes about four times longer than HD and compression does not change the time. It also says GIFs past one minute are not recommended because the export is slow, so use MP4. Version 4.1 brought a new H.264 encoder, and the vendor claims exports up to three times faster on Apple silicon. On my M1 MacBook Air in 2023 a long clip took about three times its length, which is the worst case to plan for. Clips up to 30 minutes can also go out as a shareable link instead of a file, which plays at 1080p and 60 fps.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="Do Screen Studio captions run locally or in the cloud?" group="faq">
Either. Whisper runs on your Mac (Base, Small or Medium models) on macOS 15 or earlier. Apple Speech Recognition runs locally on macOS 26 or later, where the vendor removes Whisper from the picker. Cloud transcription uploads your microphone audio, needs macOS 26 and a Screen Studio account, and is the only route that enables filler word removal. Captions are off by default and need a recorded microphone track.
</Accordion>
