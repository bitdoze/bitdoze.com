## CMR vs SMR: the one spec that actually matters

Two 3.5-inch drives can look identical on the label and behave completely differently in a NAS. The difference is how tracks are written:

- **CMR (conventional magnetic recording):** tracks are laid side by side. Writing data only touches the track being written. Random writes (which RAID parity updates and ZFS copy-on-write generate constantly) run at full speed.
- **SMR (shingled magnetic recording):** tracks overlap like roof shingles to squeeze more capacity onto the same platters. Rewriting one track means rewriting its neighbors, so the drive has to pause and restructure data. Sustained write speeds collapse to single-digit MB/s.

That collapse is exactly what happens during a **RAID rebuild or ZFS resilver**. People on TrueNAS and Synology forums have documented SMR drives taking *weeks* to resilver, dropping out of the array mid-rebuild, or getting kicked by the controller for unresponsiveness, at which point a second drive failure becomes a dead pool. SMR drives also pay this penalty forever, not just during rebuilds: every heavy write session on a busy Plex or backup box stalls.

WD got caught quietly shipping SMR in the Red line in 2020 and had to rename the product line in response. That's why **Red Plus and Red Pro are guaranteed CMR** while plain Red is not. Seagate kept IronWolf and Exos CMR throughout. Toshiba's N300 is CMR as well.

Rule of thumb: if a listing doesn't explicitly say CMR, assume SMR and skip it for NAS use.

## How many drives do you actually need?

RAID levels trade capacity for redundancy, and the math changes what you should buy:

| Drives | RAID level | Usable (12TB disks) | Survives |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | Mirror / RAID 1 | 12TB | 1 disk |
| 3 | RAID 5 / RAIDZ1 | 24TB | 1 disk |
| 4 | RAID 5 / RAIDZ1 | 36TB | 1 disk |
| 4 | RAID 6 / RAIDZ2 | 24TB | 2 disks |
| 4 | RAID 10 | 24TB | 1–2 disks |

Two honest opinions here:

- **RAID 5 with large drives is a real risk.** A 20TB rebuild takes a day or more and hammers the surviving disks the whole time. With 16TB+ drives, use RAID 6/RAIDZ2 (or mirrors); the second parity disk is cheap insurance compared to a double failure mid-resilver.
- **RAID is not backup.** It protects against disk death, not ransomware, fat fingers, or the house burning down. Keep a copy off the array; even a [USB DAS](/best-das-enclosure-homelab/) doing nightly syncs counts.

For Plex libraries specifically, a big single-volume pool of media you can re-rip is a different risk profile than family photos. I run RAIDZ2 on irreplaceable data and a plain large pool for media. The [NAS for Plex guide](/best-nas-for-plex/) covers the enclosure side.

## Buying tips that save real money

- **Buy from different batches.** Drives from the same manufacturing batch share failure patterns. If you're buying 3–4 disks, split the order across two listings, brands, or a couple of weeks of purchases. It's cheap diversity.
- **Run a burn-in test before trusting the pool.** `badblocks -wsv` or a full SMART extended test over the first week catches most infant mortality. Better a DOA return than a rebuild.
- **Be honest about recertified drives.** Factory-recertified Exos and IronWolf drives sell for 20–30% less (including the $798.99 Exos X24 listing above, or direct from ServerPartDeals and goHardDrive), usually with shorter seller warranties (1–3 years) and no Seagate Rescue plan. They're a reasonable gamble for redundant media pools, and a bad idea for your only copy of family photos. Always check the warranty length and seller on the listing itself.
- **Shucking is mostly dead.** For years the cheapest NAS drives were WD Elements/My Book externals containing white-label CMR helium drives. Prices on externals have risen and internal stock has improved, so this barely pays in 2026, and you lose the warranty the moment the case comes off. Only worth it if you find a clearance deal.
- **Match drive count to bays, not just capacity.** Four 12TB drives in RAIDZ2 give 24TB usable. Two 24TB Exos mirrored give the same, but leave two empty bays for later expansion (or pair with a [DAS](/best-das-enclosure-homelab/) down the road). Plan for the NAS you'll have in three years.

## FAQ

<Accordion label="Is IronWolf or WD Red better for a NAS?" group="faq" expanded="true">
They're equivalent at the same tier: IronWolf competes with Red Plus, IronWolf Pro with Red Pro. Both are 7200rpm, CMR, and rated for 180TB/year (standard) or 300TB/year (Pro). In late 2026 the IronWolf simply costs less per TB, which is why it's the default pick. Buy whichever is cheaper per TB on the day you order.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="Can I use Exos datacenter drives in a home NAS?" group="faq">
Yes. They're standard SATA drives and work fine in UGREEN, QNAP, TerraMaster, and TrueNAS builds. One exception to check: Synology's 2025/2026 `x25+` series (like the DS925+) enforces a verified drive compatibility list that flags or blocks unverified third-party drives (see our [UGREEN vs Synology guide](/ugreen-vs-synology/)). Beyond compatibility lists, the trade-offs with Exos are acoustic (loud seeks) and power (roughly double the idle watts of a desktop NAS drive). Put them in a rack or a room you don't sit in and they're the best $/TB in this lineup.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="Why is SMR bad for RAID and ZFS?" group="faq">
SMR drives rewrite whole shingled track groups for a small change, so sustained writes collapse. During a RAID rebuild or ZFS resilver (a multi-hour sustained write), SMR disks can stall for so long the controller marks them dead, and a second disk failing mid-rebuild kills the pool. CMR has no such penalty. Never put SMR in a redundant array.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="Should all drives in my NAS be the same model?" group="faq">
Same capacity, ideally. Mixed capacities work (especially in Synology SHR or MergerFS) but standard RAID uses the smallest disk in the pool. Same *model* isn't required, and buying from different batches or even brands slightly reduces correlated-failure risk. Don't mix CMR and SMR drives in a pool, and don't mix dramatically different speeds.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="Are recertified or renewed NAS drives worth it?" group="faq">
Sometimes. Factory-recertified and Amazon Renewed enterprise drives (like the $798.99 Exos X24 listing above) save 20–30% but carry shorter seller warranties, typically 1–3 years instead of Seagate's 5-year retail coverage, and no Rescue data-recovery plan. They're defensible for redundant media libraries you can re-create; skip them for the pool holding things you can't.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="How much usable space do I get from 4× 12TB in RAID?" group="faq">
RAID 5 / RAIDZ1: ~36TB usable (one disk of parity). RAID 6 / RAIDZ2: ~24TB usable (two disks of parity). RAID 10: ~24TB usable. With 12TB+ drives I recommend RAID 6/RAIDZ2 for irreplaceable data because rebuild times are long enough that a second failure during resilver is a real possibility, not a hypothetical.
</Accordion>
