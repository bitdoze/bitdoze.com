## What is SureCart?

SureCart is a WordPress eCommerce plugin built for digital products, subscriptions, and services. It is not trying to replace WooCommerce for physical goods. If you need complex shipping rules or large inventory management, look elsewhere. That said, physical support has grown: shipping zones and rates, inventory tracking, returns and refunds, plus Shippo integration for buying and printing shipping labels from the order screen.

The architecture is the reason to care. Checkout, payments, subscriptions, tax calculation, the customer portal, and file delivery run on SureCart's servers. Your WordPress site renders the frontend with blocks. No order tables in your database, no payment logic in your stack. When a sale happens, your server is not doing the heavy lifting.

<Image src={svgArchitecture} alt="SureCart headless architecture: the browser loads pages from WordPress, while checkout, payments, subscriptions, tax and file delivery run in the SureCart cloud" />

The trade-off: product pages load SureCart's scripts, and your store data lives on their platform. The script part got a lot better in 2026 (more in the performance section below). The data part is now much easier to exit (see data portability at the end).

If you are setting up a fresh store, you need WordPress running first. I have guides for [installing WordPress with Docker Compose](/install-wordpress-docker/) and [installing WordPress on Ubuntu ARM with CloudPanel](/install-wordpress-on-ubuntu-arm/).

## SureCart pricing and fees (2026)

SureCart's pricing model is one free plan and three paid tiers that differ only in store count and transaction fees. Every plan gets every feature. SureCart states this plainly on the [pricing page](https://surecart.com/pricing/) (accessed November 2026): same features on all plans, from free to Pro Unlimited, no feature restrictions.

| Plan | Annual | Lifetime | SureCart transaction fee | Stores |
|------|--------|----------|--------------------------|--------|
| Launch (free) | $0 | N/A | 2.9% per transaction | 1 |
| Pro | $199/yr | $699 | None | 1 |
| Pro 5 | $299/yr | $1,199 | None | 5 |
| Pro Unlimited | $499/yr | $1,999 | None | Unlimited |

**Launch (free)**: $0 with no product or revenue limits, but SureCart takes a 2.9% transaction fee on every sale, on top of whatever your payment processor charges.

**Pro tiers** kill the SureCart fee entirely. All prices are USD regular prices as of November 2026.

<Notice type="warning" title="The free tier fee went up">
The free tier's cut went from 1.9% to 2.9% in autumn 2026, a 53% jump. Official plugin readmes bracket the change between September 15 and October 20, 2026. Every older review (including the first version of this one) still quotes 1.9%.
</Notice>

<Notice type="info" title="Pricing caveats">
Prices above are regular prices as of November 2026. The old standing "intro pricing" is gone; the current deal structure is "20% off your first year" with renewals at full price. SureCart runs promos a few times a year (Black Friday has been 20% off annual and 40% off lifetime), so check the pricing page the day you buy and don't plan around sale numbers. Paid plans carry a 14-day money-back guarantee.
</Notice>

<Picture formats={["webp"]} fallbackFormat="webp" src={imgPricing} alt="SureCart pricing plans 2026: free Launch plan with 2.9% fee and Pro tiers" />

What hides behind "extra charge" is small: team accounts (extra seats for staff) and a custom white-label sender email. Products, subscriptions, payment gateways, checkout features, the affiliate platform, the licensing system, and automatic tax are on every plan.

That last one deserves a cost note. Automatic tax calculation (EU VAT, US sales tax, Canadian GST/HST) runs through TaxJar and SureCart pays for the calculations. Standalone tax engines for WordPress cost around $50/month. If you sell into the EU, this alone can justify the platform choice.

### The 2.9% free-plan transaction fee

On the Launch plan, SureCart adds 2.9% per transaction on top of your payment processor's fees. Upgrade to any Pro plan and the SureCart fee drops to zero. You still pay your processor.

Here is what the total take looks like on a typical small sale:

    | Launch (free) | €0.87 | €0.70 | €1.57 | ~5.2% |

Processor fees apply on every plan. Only the SureCart slice disappears on Pro.

<Notice type="info" title="The 'SureCart Application Fee' line in Stripe">
On subscription products you will see a "SureCart Application Fee" line item in Stripe Billing. That is Stripe's subscription fee being redirected, not a second SureCart charge stacked on top. SureCart documents this in their Stripe application fee doc specifically so operators don't panic when reconciling payouts.
</Notice>

For context on the 2.9% number itself: SureCart's [fees documentation](https://surecart.com/docs/understanding-ecommerce-fees/) (accessed November 2026) compares it against Easy Digital Downloads at 3%, GiveWP at 2%, and Lemon Squeezy taking 5% on recovered carts. 2.9% is mid-pack. Where your breakeven lands matters more than whether 2.9% is fair.

### Free vs Pro: where the breakeven lands

Pro at $199/year versus a 2.9% fee is simple arithmetic: $199 ÷ 0.029 = **~$6,900 in annual revenue**. Below that, the free plan is cheaper. Above it, Pro pays for itself.

At the old 1.9% fee the breakeven sat around $10,500, so the fee hike pulled the crossover about 35% closer.

| Annual revenue | Launch cost (2.9% fee) | Pro cost ($199/yr) | Cheaper |
|----------------|------------------------|--------------------|---------|
| $1,000 | $29 | $199 | Launch |
| $7,000 | $203 | $199 | Pro (breakeven ≈ $6,900) |
| $20,000 | $580 | $199 | Pro |

Quick decision rule:

  <ul>

  </ul>

Run the table on your own numbers. At $40,000/yr on the free plan you are paying roughly $1,160 in fees to avoid a $199/year cost. At $500/yr the fee is $14.50 and the free plan is obviously fine.

## Payment processors

<Picture formats={["webp"]} fallbackFormat="webp" src={imgPayments} alt="SureCart supported payment processors: Stripe, PayPal, Mollie, Razorpay and Paystack" />

Stripe, PayPal, Mollie, Razorpay, and Paystack, plus manual/bank-transfer methods. Multiple processors can run on the same checkout form, which matters if you sell across regions: Stripe for cards and wallets, Mollie for iDEAL and Bancontact in the Benelux, Razorpay for India (added in 3.19.0, February 2026), Paystack for African markets.

SureCart advertises 100+ payment methods across 135+ currencies and 45+ countries. Through Stripe you also get Apple Pay, Google Pay, wallets, and bank debits on top of cards.

Two operator notes. First, processor fees stack on top of SureCart's fee, so the total-take math in the tabs above is the number that matters. SureCart's docs quote US Stripe rates (2.9% + 30¢); EU/EEA sellers pay Stripe's lower European rates, so your total take is better than the docs' examples suggest.

Second, payment method availability depends on your processor account, not just SureCart. iDEAL needs Mollie. Local Indian methods need Razorpay. If a method is missing at checkout, check the processor dashboard first and SureCart settings second. Mollie + iDEAL shows up often enough in support threads to be worth knowing before you go looking for a bug.

### Software licensing for plugin and theme sellers

If you sell WordPress plugins or themes, read this part twice. SureCart includes a full licensing platform: license key generation, a validation and activation API, and update distribution for your code. No third-party licensing plugin, no per-site licensing fee.

<Notice type="success" title="Included on all plans">
The licensing system is part of the product on every plan, including the free one. Standalone licensing add-ons for other platforms run $99-$199/year on top of the store software.
</Notice>

The workflow for a plugin seller is straightforward: create a product with a license key attached, sell it through a SureCart checkout, deliver the zip through SureCart's file delivery, and call the activation API from your plugin's update checker. Distribution and auto-updates for your customers are included.

This is what sets SureCart apart if you ship code. Sell your plugin on SureCart, deliver updates through the licensing API.

This is the comparison most people need. SureCart has grown real physical-product support (shipping zones and rates, inventory tracking, returns, Shippo labels) but it's still not WooCommerce once physical operations get complex. For digital products and subscriptions it usually wins on maintenance cost alone.

## Checkout, order bumps, and upsells

Checkout forms are built with blocks: add your product, choose which fields to include, embed the form on any page. Order bumps, upsells, and coupon fields are part of the builder.

<Picture formats={["webp"]} fallbackFormat="webp" src={imgBlocks} alt="SureCart checkout form built with WordPress blocks" />

Since 4.12 (November 2026) you also get customizable instant-checkout pages at `/buy/` product URLs, editable in the Site Editor. The 4.10 and 4.11 releases added Starter Templates and a Patterns library (product page, quick view, sticky purchase bar, upsell patterns), so you are not starting from an empty page.

Revenue features that cost extra elsewhere are built in: rules-based order bumps (multiple at once, now also in the slide-out cart), one-click post-purchase upsells with countdown, Price Boost, cart-abandonment recovery emails with discounts, and expiring-card reminders for failed subscription payments.

<Notice type="info" title="Your WordPress install still needs SMTP">
SureCart sends its own transactional receipts from its infrastructure, but your WordPress site still sends its own mail (password resets, comment notifications, admin alerts). Set up [sending WordPress emails through Zoho SMTP with FluentSMTP](/send-emails-in-wordpress-zoho-smtp-fluentsmtp/) so those don't land in spam.
</Notice>

And since the store admin processes real orders, put [two-factor authentication for your WordPress admin](/secure-wordpress-2fa/) before you take payments. Both jobs take minutes. Skip them and you get to find out why everyone recommends them the hard way.

## Integrations and automation

LMS and membership platforms: LearnDash, LifterLMS, TutorLMS, Masteriyo, GamiPress, SureMembers, and BuddyBoss are all on SureCart's [integrations page](https://surecart.com/integrations/) (accessed November 2026). Shipping: Shippo for buying and printing labels straight from the order screen.

Automation runs through two paths. OttoKit automation (cloud service from the same team, formerly SureTriggers) connects SureCart events to hundreds of apps: purchases, abandonment, and renewals into your CRM or email tool. Zapier is also a listed integration if you're already in that ecosystem, with its 5,000+ apps. Both are cloud services, not self-hosted.

Whichever automation tool you pick, the common pattern is pushing SureCart events (new purchase, cart abandoned, subscription renewing) into your email platform. I keep costs down with [affordable email marketing tools like EmailOctopus](https://go.bitdoze.com/emailoctopus) for exactly this.

SureCart also ships a native affiliate platform: affiliate dashboard, coupon codes, commission overrides, and payout management. A 2026 improvement routes click tracking through your own domain so ad blockers don't silently drop affiliate clicks. This is built in, not a paid add-on.

### AI-ready catalog and MCP

4.13.0 added what SureCart calls an Agent-Ready Catalog: a public read-only product feed designed for shopping AIs (ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot, Perplexity). Your products become machine-readable data for agents whether you asked for it or not.

<Notice type="warning" title="On by default">
The agent catalog feed is enabled out of the box. If you don't want your catalog public in agent-readable form, turn it off at Settings → AI Assistants. Takes ten seconds.
</Notice>

There is also a "Connect your AI" wizard: a 4-step MCP (Model Context Protocol) setup that lets Claude, ChatGPT, Cursor, or VS Code read and manage your store, with separate switches for read, edit, and delete permissions. If you run coding agents against your store, this is genuinely useful and the permission switches are the right design.

<Notice type="info" title="ChatGPT connection is time-limited">
ChatGPT custom GPTs are being retired on December 11, 2026, which kills the ChatGPT MCP connection. The Claude, Cursor, and VS Code connections keep working. This is a platform risk on OpenAI's side, not SureCart's.
</Notice>

### Performance and cache plugin compatibility

My original review's biggest complaint: SureCart's scripts on product pages add weight you don't control. That complaint is mostly historical now. Versions 4.11 and 4.13 added automatic cache compatibility: SureCart configures exclusions for WP Super Cache, Autoptimize, Breeze, Cache Enabler, Hummingbird, WP-Optimize, NitroPack, Fast Velocity Minify, Flying Pages, Asset CleanUp, Perfmatters, and SiteGround Optimizer without manual work. Checkout and customer-dashboard pages are excluded from caching and delayed JavaScript. Cached product pages get purged when stock changes. LiteSpeed Cache has its own doc.

<Notice type="success" title="Mostly auto-fixed since 4.11">
The script-weight downside is largely handled for common cache stacks. Still verify on your stack: run a Lighthouse check on a product page with your cache plugin active before you commit. Combinations differ, and a script that gets along with WP Rocket can still fight a Cloudflare + Varnish + minification setup.
</Notice>

If you're tuning a WordPress stack around this, my guides to [speed up WordPress with Cloudflare, Varnish and Redis](/speed-up-wordpress-with-cloudflare-varnish-and-redis/) and [setting up Varnish caching with CloudPanel](/cloudpanel-varnish-cache/) cover the two setups I run most often.

The upside holds: because transactions never hit your server, a small VPS handles sale-day traffic you'd otherwise size for. Your box serves pages, SureCart's cloud takes the payment load.

## Data portability and the headless trade-off

The honest risk of headless commerce: your store data lives on someone else's servers. When I first reviewed SureCart that was a hard stop for some readers. The picture is better now.

SureCart has a Settings → Export Data screen that produces CSVs of customers, products, purchases, and subscriptions. Bulk CSV imports exist for the same entities. There is a WooCommerce importer and a store-transfer tool if you move accounts. Your Stripe account is yours: customers and payment methods sit in your Stripe, not in SureCart.

<Notice type="warning" title="Where the real lock-in sits">
Active subscriptions live on SureCart's platform. Recurring billing history and subscriber state don't export as cleanly as one-time orders. Migrating a live subscription base means re-subscribing customers or re-creating billing agreements elsewhere. That's the part you can't undo in an afternoon.
</Notice>

Everything else you can get out. And your WordPress site still needs its own backups even though store data lives in the cloud: pages, media, theme, plugin config. See my roundup of [free WordPress backup plugins](/best-free-wordpress-backup-plugins/) and run one on a schedule. Losing your content hurts more than losing order history. Order history is in the export.

## Is SureCart worth it?

Yes, if you sell digital products, subscriptions, or services on WordPress and want zero store infrastructure to maintain. The decision is mostly the breakeven math, not vibes.

Under roughly $7,000/year in revenue the free tier wins: 2.9% of a small number is small, and you get every feature. Above that, the $199/year Pro plan pays for itself and keeps paying. If you ship plugins or themes, the licensing platform is included either way, which is worth $99-$199/year versus standalone licensing add-ons.

Think twice if: you need complex shipping or large physical inventory (go WooCommerce, comparison above); you can't tolerate any external script on product pages; or absolute ownership of recurring billing matters to your risk model.

If you'd rather not run WordPress at all, hosted storefronts are an option. I have a guide to [selling digital products online with Payhip](/sell-digital-products-payhip/) if you want to [start a free Payhip storefront](https://go.bitdoze.com/payhip) without running a site. On lifetime: $699 for one store is reasonable if you're committed for 3+ years, since it beats $199/year starting in year four. Lifetime prices went up in 2026 and may go up again, so if you're sure, buying earlier is cheaper.

## FAQ

<Accordion label="Is SureCart really free?" group="faq" expanded="true">
Yes. The Launch plan is $0 with no product or revenue limits and no time limit. The catch is a 2.9% transaction fee on every sale, on top of your payment processor's fees. For low-volume sellers that's a few dollars a month. At higher volume the fee exceeds the $199/year Pro plan at about $6,900 of annual revenue.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="Does SureCart charge transaction fees?" group="faq">
Only on the free Launch plan: 2.9% per transaction as of autumn 2026 (up from 1.9% earlier in the year). All Pro plans drop the SureCart fee entirely. You still pay your payment processor (Stripe, PayPal, Mollie, Razorpay, or Paystack) on every plan.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="Is SureCart better than WooCommerce?" group="faq">
For digital products, subscriptions, and services: usually yes. Less to maintain, no order-table bloat on your server, and licensing, affiliate tracking, and automatic tax built in. For complex shipping rules, large physical inventory, or a hard requirement to own every byte of store data in your own database: WooCommerce. SureCart has added real physical support (Shippo labels, inventory, returns) but it's not WooCommerce depth.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="What payment gateways does SureCart support?" group="faq">
Stripe, PayPal, Mollie, Razorpay (India), and Paystack, plus manual and bank-transfer methods. Multiple gateways can run on the same checkout form. SureCart advertises 100+ payment methods across 135+ currencies and 45+ countries; what appears at checkout depends on your processor accounts.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="Can I migrate away from SureCart?" group="faq">
For catalog, customers, and order history: yes. Settings → Export Data gives you CSVs, there are bulk CSV imports, a WooCommerce importer, and a store-transfer tool. Your payment methods live in your Stripe account. Active subscriptions are the exception: recurring billing state lives on SureCart's platform, and migrating live subscribers means re-subscribing them elsewhere.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="Does SureCart handle EU VAT?" group="faq">
Yes. Automatic tax calculation including EU VAT, US sales tax, and Canadian GST/HST runs through TaxJar, and SureCart pays for the calculations on every plan. You configure tax settings once; rates resolve at checkout.
</Accordion>
