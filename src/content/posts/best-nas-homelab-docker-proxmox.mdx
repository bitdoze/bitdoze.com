## How I picked these

I started with every NAS on Amazon shipping an x86 CPU, 8GB+ RAM (or an upgrade slot), at least one M.2 NVMe bay, and 2.5GbE networking, then cut anything above $1,300 diskless. ARM-only units are out (most VM images and a chunk of container images won't run), and so are boxes with soldered, non-upgradable RAM under 8GB.

What's left is a short list. I evaluated each on four things: CPU performance per dollar, RAM ceiling, out-of-box networking speed, and whether the OS lets you run Docker and VMs without hacks. I also weighed the option of skipping a NAS entirely and building a mini PC + DAS; for some homelabs that's the better call.

## What actually matters for a Docker/Proxmox NAS

- **RAM headroom.** Docker containers and a couple of VMs eat RAM faster than CPU. 8GB is the floor; 32GB is comfortable. Non-upgradable RAM (looking at you, soldered models) is a hard stop.
- **An NVMe slot for apps.** Spinning disks are for bulk storage. Container volumes, databases, and VM disks belong on the M.2 slots every pick here includes.
- **2.5GbE minimum.** Containers serving your LAN and Proxmox nodes pulling VM disks over NFS saturate 1GbE instantly. 10GbE future-proofs the link to your main server and the [switch](/best-2-5-10gbe-switch-homelab/) everything hangs off.
- **x86 CPU.** ARM NAS units are cheaper but rule out most VM images and some container images. Every pick below is x86.
- **Transcoding.** If Jellyfin/Plex lives on the NAS, you want Intel QuickSync, which the Synology's Ryzen and the DXP4800 GT's Ryzen don't have. If it doesn't have to live on the NAS, a small Intel box handles it: see the [best mini PC for Plex and Jellyfin](/best-mini-pc-plex-jellyfin/) and the [best NAS for Plex](/best-nas-for-plex/) guide, which ranks these same boxes by transcode ability.

## Power draw and noise: measured numbers

These boxes run 24/7, so watts are a real line item. At $0.17/kWh, 10W of continuous draw is about $15 a year. Here's what the numbers look like across the lineup (mix of vendor-published and measured figures):

| N150 mini PC + D4-320 | ~10W | ~15-25W | ~45W | fanless PC + DAS fan | measured class |

The honest takeaway: they're all close, and the drives matter more than the box. Four IronWolfs at ~5W idle each add ~20W to whatever the NAS itself draws, and four Exos drives add ~35W. On throughput, the other number people ask about: a 10GbE link to the DXP4800 Plus or GT moves ~1,100MB/s when the pool sits on NVMe. On spinning disks you're drive-limited to ~280MB/s, which is exactly what 2.5GbE carries anyway. 10GbE only earns its keep once NVMe or several clients enter the picture.

## Drives to buy with it

Drives are now the expensive *two-thirds* of a NAS. The median NAS drive went from ~$21/TB in mid-2024 to ~$50/TB this November, up 144% as AI datacenters absorbed supply. An 8TB Red Plus that sold for $175 last September is about $355 today. Three rules before the table:

- **CMR only.** SMR drives (plain WD Red, most desktop drives) take far longer to rebuild a RAID array or resilver a ZFS pool, and a slow rebuild is when a second drive fails. Seagate IronWolf and IronWolf Pro, WD Red Plus and Red Pro, and Toshiba N300 are CMR lines.
- **Buy for the capacity you'll need in three years.** Bays are the scarce resource, not dollars per TB.
- **Read the seller line.** A drive shipped by Amazon goes through Amazon returns; a drive shipped by the seller doesn't.

## Or skip the NAS: mini PC + DAS

Before buying a NAS at all, price the alternative: an N150 mini PC running Proxmox or plain Docker, plus a [USB DAS enclosure](/best-das-enclosure-homelab/) for the bulk disks. On Nov 18 that was a [Beelink EQ14](/go/beelink-eq14/) at $379 (16GB, dual 2.5GbE; more options in the [home server mini PC guide](/best-mini-pc-home-server/)) and a [TerraMaster D4-320](/go/terramaster-d4-320/) at $189.99 (4 bays over 10Gbps USB-C, 4.2★ from 409 ratings). That's about $569 diskless, against $620 for the DXP4800 Plus. Our [NAS build under $500 guide](/nas-build-under-500/) works through the DIY option in detail, including a Jonsbo N3 + N100 board build that lands under the mini-PC price.

| | Appliance NAS | Mini PC + DAS |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Setup effort | Hours | A weekend |
| Compute headroom | Limited | Whatever you buy |
| Drive management | Polished (SHR/RAID GUI) | You're the RAID admin (ZFS/mdadm) |
| Power | ~20-35W | ~10-15W + enclosure |
| Best when | You want a storage appliance | You want one Proxmox cluster anyway |

If you already run [a Proxmox mini PC](/best-mini-pc-proxmox/) or plan to, a NAS appliance is mostly redundant. Attach storage to the cluster and be done. Buy the NAS when you want storage to be a separate, boring, always-on appliance that survives your cluster experiments.

One caveat with the DAS route: a USB enclosure puts a USB bridge between the disks and the OS. ZFS and mdadm work on it, but a flaky bridge shows up as disks dropping out of the pool. On day one, check that SMART data comes through (`smartctl -a /dev/sdX`) and leave the pool under load for a few hours before you trust it with data.

<Accordion label="What is the best NAS for a homelab in 2026?" group="faq" expanded="true">

<Accordion label="Which hard drives should I put in a NAS?" group="faq">
CMR NAS drives only: Seagate IronWolf or IronWolf Pro, WD Red Plus or Red Pro, Toshiba N300 or MG-series enterprise disks. Avoid SMR drives in any RAID or ZFS pool. In our Nov 18 check, the best living-room NAS values sat around ~$38-42/TB (Seagate IronWolf 12TB and IronWolf Pro 16TB). Renewed enterprise Exos X24 24TB drives are cheaper per TB ($33.29/TB at $798.99), though they run loud and carry a seller warranty plus a higher Q2 2026 Backblaze AFR.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="Can these NAS units run Docker containers directly?" group="faq">
Yes. All three brands support Docker natively (UGREEN and Synology have Compose-ready container managers in their OS; TerraMaster supports Docker in TOS). For heavier VM workloads, the UGREEN and TerraMaster hardware handles it out of the box; the Synology's 4GB of base RAM needs an upgrade first.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="Do I need 10GbE for a homelab NAS?" group="faq">
Not strictly. 2.5GbE saturates spinning HDDs fine (~280MB/s). 10GbE matters when you add NVMe pools, run VM disks over NFS/iSCSI, or have multiple clients hitting the NAS at once. With the DXP4800 GT at $559.99 and DXP4800 Plus at $619.99, you get 10GbE without paying a premium.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="What's the Synology drive whitelist issue?" group="faq">
The DS925+ generation only supports drives on Synology's compatibility list, which favors Synology-branded and enterprise drives and excludes many popular WD/Seagate models. It's the main reason long-time Synology users have been defecting to UGREEN and DIY builds, the [UGREEN vs Synology comparison](/ugreen-vs-synology/) goes through the whole trade-off.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="NAS or just a big USB drive on my mini PC?" group="faq">
A DAS enclosure on a mini PC is cheaper and simpler for single-server setups. Buy the NAS when you want RAID management, snapshots, and backups handled by software designed for it, or when storage needs to stay up while your compute boxes get experimented on.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="How much RAM do I need for Docker on a NAS?" group="faq">
8GB runs a handful of containers. If you're adding Immich, a Plex instance, a couple of databases, and maybe a Home Assistant VM, 32GB is where it stops being tight. That's why the F4-424 Pro shipping with 32GB matters.
</Accordion>
