## How I picked these

I started with every NAS on Amazon shipping an x86 CPU, 8GB+ RAM (or an upgrade slot), at least one M.2 NVMe bay, and 2.5GbE networking — then cut anything above $1,300 diskless. ARM-only units are out (most VM images and a chunk of container images won't run), and so are boxes with soldered, non-upgradable RAM under 8GB.

What's left is a short list. I evaluated each on four things: CPU performance per dollar, RAM ceiling, out-of-box networking speed, and whether the OS lets you run Docker and VMs without hacks. I also weighed the option of skipping a NAS entirely and building a mini PC + DAS — for some homelabs that's the better call.

## What actually matters for a Docker/Proxmox NAS

- **RAM headroom.** Docker containers and a couple of VMs eat RAM faster than CPU. 8GB is the floor; 32GB is comfortable. Non-upgradable RAM (looking at you, soldered models) is a hard stop.
- **An NVMe slot for apps.** Spinning disks are for bulk storage. Container volumes, databases, and VM disks belong on the M.2 slots every pick here includes.
- **2.5GbE minimum.** Containers serving your LAN and Proxmox nodes pulling VM disks over NFS saturate 1GbE instantly. 10GbE future-proofs the link to your main server.
- **x86 CPU.** ARM NAS units are cheaper but rule out most VM images and some container images. Every pick below is x86.
- **Transcoding.** If Jellyfin/Plex lives on the NAS, you want Intel QuickSync — which the Synology's Ryzen doesn't have.

## 3. TerraMaster F4-424 Pro — the compute king

![TerraMaster F4-424 Pro](https://m.media-amazon.com/images/I/51+zegV81qL._AC_SL1500_.jpg)

**$730.99** (list $859.99) · 4.2★, 62 reviews

The i3-N305 gives you 8 cores and 8 threads (the strongest CPU here) plus **32GB of DDR5 out of the box**. For Docker-heavy workloads that's the most capable machine in this list: the RAM you actually need is already installed.

The catch is TOS, TerraMaster's operating system. It works (Docker support exists) but it's the weakest software of the three, and TerraMaster had documented security issues in 2022–2023 — deadbolt ransomware targeted TOS vulnerabilities, and patches were slow. The community's fix is well-worn: buy the hardware, flash **TrueNAS SCALE or Unraid**, and never boot TOS. If that plan appeals to you, this is your box. If you want a turnkey appliance you'll never SSH into, look at the Synology or UGREEN instead.

## Also worth knowing

**UGREEN DXP480T Plus** (~$999): the all-flash sibling. 4x NVMe bays, no SATA, 10GbE. If your homelab data fits in ~16TB of SSD (and you value silence over raw capacity), it's a clean option. The lack of SATA bays means no cheap bulk storage, though — every TB costs flash prices.

**QNAP TS-464:** a year ago this was a contender. The Intel N5095 is showing its age against the N305 and Pentium Gold 8505 in the picks above, QNAP's software has had its own security troubles, and the price hasn't dropped to match. I'd skip it this cycle.

## The DIY option: mini PC + DAS

Before buying a NAS at all, price the alternative: a cheap mini PC ([the $130–250 N150 boxes](/best-mini-pc-home-server/) work fine) running Proxmox or plain Docker, plus a USB or Terramaster-style DAS enclosure for bulk disks.

| | Appliance NAS | Mini PC + DAS |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Setup effort | Hours | A weekend |
| Compute headroom | Limited | Whatever you buy |
| Drive management | Polished (SHR/RAID GUI) | You're the RAID admin (ZFS/mdadm) |
| Power | ~20–35W | ~10–15W + enclosure |
| Best when | You want a storage appliance | You want one Proxmox cluster anyway |

Honest take: if you already run [a Proxmox mini PC](/best-mini-pc-home-server/) or plan to, a NAS appliance is somewhat redundant. Attach storage to the cluster and be done. Buy the NAS when you want storage to be a separate, boring, always-on appliance that survives your cluster experiments.

## FAQ

<Accordion label="Can these NAS units run Docker containers directly?" group="faq">
Yes — all three support Docker natively (UGREEN and Synology have container managers in their OS; TerraMaster supports Docker in TOS). For heavier VM workloads, the UGREEN and TerraMaster hardware handles it; the Synology's 4GB of base RAM needs an upgrade first.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="Do I need 10GbE for a homelab NAS?" group="faq">
Not strictly — 2.5GbE saturates HDDs fine (~280MB/s). 10GbE matters when you add NVMe pools, run VM disks over NFS/iSCSI, or have multiple clients hitting the NAS at once. The DXP4800 Plus having it at $657 removes the dilemma.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="What's the Synology drive whitelist issue?" group="faq">
The DS925+ generation only supports drives on Synology's compatibility list, which favors Synology-branded and enterprise drives and excludes many popular WD/Seagate models. It's the main reason long-time Synology users have been defecting to UGREEN and DIY builds.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="NAS or just a big USB drive on my mini PC?" group="faq">
A DAS enclosure on a mini PC is cheaper and simpler for single-server setups. Buy the NAS when you want RAID management, snapshots, and backups handled by software designed for it — or when storage needs to stay up while your compute boxes get experimented on.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="How much RAM do I need for Docker on a NAS?" group="faq">
8GB runs a handful of containers. If you're adding Immich, a Plex instance, a couple of databases, and maybe a Home Assistant VM, 32GB is where it stops being tight — which is why the F4-424 Pro shipping with 32GB matters.
</Accordion>
