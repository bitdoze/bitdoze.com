## Which plan should you pick?

**Pro Standard** is the right answer for most people, and it's not close. The free tier and Pro Lite both lock you to `carrd.co` or `.crd.co` subdomains, which is fine for throwaway pages but looks cheap on anything you put your name on. Pro Standard is the first tier that unlocks custom domains with free SSL ([here's how to connect one](/carrd-add-domain/), takes about ten minutes).

Standard also unlocks the features that make Carrd useful beyond a static page: contact forms with 20+ email integrations, Stripe/PayPal/Gumroad widgets, code embeds, and analytics with Plausible or GA. If you're building a landing page that collects emails or takes payments, this is where it starts working.

**Pro Plus** only makes sense if you need its specific extras: advanced form actions (Zapier, Make, webhooks, Stripe Checkout), page redirects, password protection or full source export. Nice to have, rarely essential.

Skip **Pro Lite** unless you truly don't care about domains or forms. It exists mostly to remove the "Made with Carrd" footer badge.

## When Carrd is the wrong tool

Straight answer: if a one-pager isn't what you need, don't buy Carrd just because it's cheap. What I'd reach for instead, depending on the job:

- **A blog or content site** — [Astro](/build-astro-blog-free/), which is what this site runs on. It's free, it builds real multi-page sites, and my [Astro vs WordPress breakdown](/astro-vs-wordpress/) covers the actual costs. More work than Carrd, but you get a real blog out of it.
- **A one-pager without any subscription at all** — my [budget one-page website guide](/build-one-page-website-budget/) covers the free and nearly-free options.
- **A WordPress site with a visual builder** — [Breakdance](/breakdance-builder-review/) is the one I'd pick, but it needs WordPress hosting and costs several times more. Different job entirely.
- **A flat-file CMS for more than one page** — [EmDash](/emdash-cms-review/), which I've been running for six months. It's not a page builder; it's for people who want a CMS without a database.

For a landing page, a link-in-bio page or a quick client demo, though? Carrd at $11.40 a year is still the answer.
