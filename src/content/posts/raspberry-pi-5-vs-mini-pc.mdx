On November 3, 2026, Raspberry Pi raised the price of the 16GB Pi 5 by another $80 to **$385**, more than triple the $120 launch price from January 2025 for the exact same hardware. The 16GB Compute Module 5 went up $40 alongside it. Tight DRAM supply driven by AI datacenter demand is squeezing single-board computers just as hard as server memory.

At $120, buying a Pi for containers was an easy habit. At $385 for a bare board, the total bill flips the decision completely, so it is worth putting the full parts list next to what x86 mini PCs cost right now.

## The honest total: a Pi 5 is never just the board

The $385 headline is for the bare board. A Pi that actually does server work needs the rest of the costume:

| Line item | Realistic price (Nov 2026) |
|---|---|
| Raspberry Pi 5 16GB board | $385 |
| Official 27W USB-C PSU | ~$12 |
| Case + active cooler | ~$15–25 |
| NVMe HAT (SD card is not a server disk) | ~$15–30 |
| 500GB NVMe SSD | ~$45 |
| **Working total** | **~$475–500** |

Call it $475 for a 16GB ARM box with 1GbE, no hardware video encoder, and storage hanging off a PCIe HAT. Now hold that number against the alternatives.

## What the Pi still wins (and it's a real list)

None of this makes the Raspberry Pi obsolete, because its core hardware strengths never relied on having 16GB of RAM:

- **GPIO and HATs.** Sensors, relays, displays, CAN buses, and PoE HATs form a physical electronics ecosystem x86 mini PCs simply lack.
- **Native camera and display connectors.** Built-in CSI and DSI ports still beat fighting USB capture cards for robotics and kiosk builds.
- **Power and footprint.** A 3 to 4W idle draw on a credit-card board stays practical inside portable enclosures and battery projects.
- **Documentation depth.** Nearly every obscure kernel flag or pinout quirk has years of forum answers behind it.
- **Microcontrollers.** Smaller sensor duties long ago moved to $5–15 Pico and ESP32 boards, which remains the smarter split.

What disappeared from that list is using a maxed-out Pi as a general-purpose Docker server. Even power efficiency no longer rescues the budget: a Pi 5 idling around 3W versus an N150 around 7W saves roughly $7 to $10 a year at $0.20/kWh. At a $125 to $265 upfront gap, the mini PC comes out ahead for years.

</Notice>

## FAQ

<Accordion label="Will Pi prices come back down?" group="faq" expanded="true">
Raspberry Pi described the increase as temporary driven by AI datacenter DRAM demand, without giving a return date. Memory shortages push up mini PC SODIMM prices too, which is why retail listings have climbed past $300 on some N150 boxes, so buying based on today's street prices makes more sense than waiting on a rollback.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="Is ARM vs x86 still a real compatibility issue for servers?" group="faq">
Most mainstream Docker containers ship arm64 images now, though odd edge cases remain: older vendor binaries, proprietary monitoring agents, or niche containers built only for `amd64`. On x86 you never have to check the tag architecture first.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="What about the Orange Pi 5 and other ARM SBCs?" group="faq">
The Orange Pi 5 Plus (RK3588, 8 cores, 16GB, dual 2.5GbE, M.2) around $150–200 undercuts the $385 Pi 5 by more than half with faster I/O. The compromise is software polish: Raspberry Pi OS kernel support and docs remain easier to live with unless you are already comfortable maintaining Armbian installs.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="Does a mini PC idle as low as a Pi?" group="faq">
Close enough for utility bills not to decide it. A Pi 5 idles near 3–4W compared to 6–9W for an N150 box, roughly $7–10 difference per year at $0.20/kWh. Power savings only favored the Pi when the board cost $60 to $120.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="I just bought a 16GB Pi 5 before the hike. Keep it?" group="faq">
Definitely. The hardware hasn't changed, and at $120–300 you paid a fairer price than anyone buying today. Use what you already own until you actually hit a limit.
</Accordion>

## Verdict

At $385 bare (roughly $475 to $500 once you add a power supply, case, cooler, NVMe HAT, and SSD), the 16GB Raspberry Pi 5 makes sense mainly if your build requires GPIO, CSI/DSI connectors, or that exact footprint alongside 16GB of memory. For running Docker containers, Home Assistant, or a media server, an N150 box like the Beelink Mini S13 or EQ14 (or a $150 used ThinkCentre Tiny) gives you x86 compatibility, Quick Sync, and dual M.2 slots for significantly less money.
