## 1U or 2U?

1U UPSes exist but they're small batteries in a wide box: 500VA/300W class, ten-ish minutes at a real load, and mostly meant to cover graceful shutdown of a switch and one small host. If your rack is a wall-mount network rack with short depth, 1U is what fits. A full rack with a NAS and a real server wants 2U: that gets you 1500VA/1000W and batteries that hold a load long enough to matter. If you're building a small [10-inch mini rack](/best-10-inch-mini-rack/), neither fits; that's the tower-or-shelf LiFePO4 conversation below.

## Pure sine or simulated: check your power supplies first

The whole waveform argument is in the [best UPS for a home server](/best-ups-for-home-server/) guide, and the short version is this: any PSU with active power factor correction (every 80 Plus PSU, every NAS brick worth owning) can shut down on the stepped "simulated sine" output of cheap UPSes, right at the moment you needed the UPS. For servers and NAS boxes, pure sine is not negotiable.

The twist in rack form: several popular 1U units output simulated sine. The CyberPower OR500LCDRM1U and OR700LCDRM1U are that case ([CyberPower's own pages list simulated sine wave](https://www.cyberpowersystems.com/product/ups/smart-app-lcd/or500lcdrm1u/)), which makes them fine for switches, modems and routers, and wrong for the server. Tripp Lite's datasheets describe battery-mode output as "PWM sine wave," which is not the pure sine claim it sounds like ([SMART1500LCD datasheet](https://assets.tripplite.com/product-pdfs/en/smart1500lcd.pdf)).

## LiFePO4: longer battery life, rarely in rack form

A lead-acid UPS needs new batteries every 3-5 years. LiFePO4 packs are rated for thousands of cycles and a decade, so cost-per-year of battery life favors lithium even at higher upfront prices. The problem: rack-mount LiFePO4 UPSes at homelab prices are rare; the segment is mostly towers.

The workaround is the [GOLDENMATE 1000VA/800W](/go/goldenmate-1000va-lifepo4/) at $229.48 (4.5★/89): a 230.4Wh LiFePO4 unit whose listing claims pure sine wave output, with a USB monitoring port it says triggers safe auto-shutdown on PCs and NAS devices. It's a tower that sits on a rack shelf, not a rack unit. If you want shelf space and chemistry both, that's the current answer.

The battery math is why I keep bringing it up. Sealed lead-acid needs replacement every 3-5 years whether the UPS ever discharges or not (per [APC's FAQ](https://www.se.com/us/en/faqs/FA158934/)). Prices checked October 23, 2026: the CP1500PFCRM2U takes the RB1290X2D cartridge at about $124 on Amazon, and the SMT1500RM2UC takes the APCRBC133, which runs roughly $150-225 depending on source. Call it $25-45 a year for the CyberPower and $30-75 for the APC over a decade, on top of the purchase price. The GOLDENMATE's LiFePO4 pack is rated for years longer and isn't a user-replaceable cartridge, so its ~$230 is closer to the real ten-year number. Battery-shop generic 12V/9Ah pairs undercut the cartridges but void the clean-swapping convenience.

For multi-hour outages there's the [EcoFlow DELTA 3](/go/ecoflow-delta-3/) at $629 (4.7★/1,383): a 1024Wh power station with UPS mode. The transfer-time and pass-through caveats are covered in the [home server UPS guide](/best-ups-for-home-server/); the short version is test the switchover once by pulling the plug.

## Automatic shutdown: NUT, USB and SNMP

A UPS without shutdown signaling is a delay, not protection. The open standard path is Network UPS Tools: any HID-class USB UPS that NUT's `usbhid-ups` driver supports works the same way, which covers the CyberPower and APC units here and likely the GOLDENMATE. Check the [NUT hardware compatibility list](https://networkupstools.org/stable-hcl.html) for your exact model before assuming.

The minimal server side, covered fully in the [best UPS for a home server](/best-ups-for-home-server/) guide:

```ini
[homeups]
    driver = usbhid-ups
    port = auto
    desc = "Rack UPS"
```

Clients: TrueNAS has a built-in UPS service, Proxmox hosts run NUT directly on the host (the [Proxmox mini PC guide](/best-mini-pc-proxmox/) covers the host), and Synology DSM can act as a client of a NUT server using the documented UPS name and credentials (`ups` / `monuser` / `secret`). For UniFi gear, I can't find a documented UniFi NUT client path; assume UniFi gear just rides it out.

For network management instead of USB, the CyberPower units take an optional RMCARD205/305 slot card. The SMT1500RM2UC comes with SmartConnect Ethernet monitoring built in, and its SmartSlot takes an AP9630/31 card if you want the full network management feature set.

<Accordion label="1U or 2U rack UPS?" group="faq">

<Accordion label="Does a NAS need a pure sine UPS?" group="faq">
Yes. NAS power bricks and server PSUs use active PFC, which can shut down on simulated-sine output. For a NAS, treat pure sine as a requirement and simulated sine as network-gear-only.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="How long will a rack UPS run a homelab?" group="faq">
At a typical 60-120W load, a 1500VA unit gives roughly 45-90 minutes and a 500VA 1U gives 10-20 minutes, based on vendor curves. The UPS's real job is a clean shutdown; runtime past that is bonus.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="Is a LiFePO4 UPS worth it?" group="faq">
On battery economics, yes: LiFePO4 cells outlast lead-acid by years and thousands of cycles. The catch is rack-mount LiFePO4 barely exists at homelab prices, so it means a tower unit on a shelf today.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="Can a UPS shut down TrueNAS, Synology or Proxmox?" group="faq">
Yes, through NUT. TrueNAS has a UPS service, Proxmox runs NUT on the host, and Synology DSM can be a NUT client using the documented network UPS name and credentials. USB into the host, clients over the LAN.
</Accordion>
