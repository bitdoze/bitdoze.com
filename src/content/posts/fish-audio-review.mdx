## Is Fish Audio free?

Yes, in two different ways, and the difference matters:

- **The web app free tier** gives you 8,000 credits every month, no credit card needed (see the [Fish Audio plan page](https://fish.audio/plan/)). One minute of generated speech costs roughly 600 credits, so that is about 7 minutes of audio per month, in chunks of up to 500 characters. You get 3 public voice slots and standard generation speed. No commercial use, no enhanced voice cloning.
- **The free API model** is separate from the web credits. Set `model: "s2.1-pro-free"` in your API calls and you get the same S2.1 Pro model at $0, under fair-use limits, per the [models overview](https://docs.fish.audio/developer-guide/models-pricing/models-overview). No TTFA (time to first audio) or DPA guarantees, which is the trade. For testing, prototyping, and smaller projects it is hard to argue with.

## The models: S2.1 Pro, drama-3, and what happened to S1

Fish Audio runs three current TTS models plus one on the way out:

| Model | Price | Best for | Notes |
|-------|-------|----------|-------|
| `s2.1-pro` | $15 / million UTF-8 bytes | Production TTS | TTFA and DPA guarantees, 83 languages |
| `s2.1-pro-free` | $0 | Development and small projects | Same model, no guarantees, fair-use limits |
| `drama-3` | $50 / million UTF-8 bytes | Characters and emotional scripts | Maximum expressiveness, newest flagship |
| `s1` | retired Dec 31, 2026 | nothing new | Deprecated; requests get served by s2.1-pro after the date |

One million UTF-8 bytes is roughly 180,000 English words or about 12 hours of speech, so $15 of s2.1-pro goes a long way.

The emotion control syntax changed with the S2 generation. The old S1 used parenthesis tags like `(excited)` from a fixed list. S2.1 Pro and S2 Pro use natural language in **brackets**: `[whispers sweetly]`, `[laughing nervously]`, or whatever description fits. It is not limited to a fixed set, which makes it far more flexible and a little less predictable. Most TTS tools give you a flat monotone output unless you fight with SSML. Here you write what you want and the delivery changes. It makes a real difference when you narrate a 10-minute video and need the tone to shift.

S2 Pro is the one to know if you want to run things yourself. The [S2 Pro model card](https://huggingface.co/fishaudio/s2-pro) publishes the weights, fine-tuning code and an SGLang-based inference engine, but under the [Fish Audio Research License](https://huggingface.co/fishaudio/s2-pro/blob/main/LICENSE.md): research and non-commercial use is free, commercial use needs a separate written license. That is open weights, not open source in the usual sense. The drama-3 weights are also on [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/fishaudio/drama-3), under the Fish Audio Community License. I compared the main open weights in [the open-source TTS engines roundup](/best-open-source-tts/).

## What Fish Audio does well

**Voice cloning is fast.** I recorded about 15 seconds of myself reading a paragraph, uploaded it, and had a working clone in under two minutes. For comparison, ElevenLabs [documents](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/creative-platform/voices/voice-cloning/instant-voice-cloning) a 10-second minimum for Instant Voice Cloning and recommends 1 to 2 minutes of clean audio, and its Starter plan already includes instant cloning. So the gap is shorter sample requirements, not a paywall. The S2.1 Pro model supports 83 languages, though clone quality varies by language.

![Decorative "Clone Your Voice" graphic with a microphone icon and an AI CLONING badge](../../assets/images/26/07/fish-audio-clone-voice.webp)

**Multilingual support is strong.** I tested it with mixed English and Romanian content and the pronunciation was clean, without the robotic accent bleed you get from some other tools. S2.1 Pro lists [83 languages](https://docs.fish.audio/developer-guide/models-pricing/models-overview), while the Fish Audio landing page still says 30+. ElevenLabs now lists 90+ languages for Eleven v4 and 70+ for v3 on its [API pricing page](https://elevenlabs.io/pricing/api), so language count is no longer a reason to pick Fish. Test your own language in both.

**The community voice library is large.** The [voice library](https://fish.audio/voice-library) advertises over 2,000,000 voices, so you will probably find something close to what you need without cloning anything. You can search by language, style, and use case. Some of the community voices are surprisingly good.

**The API pricing is simple.** Pay as you go, no subscription required for API access. TTS is billed per million UTF-8 bytes of input text, transcription is $0.36 per audio hour, and errors are not billed. Rate limits work on concurrency, not QPS: 5 concurrent requests until you have prepaid $100 total, then 15, then 50 at $1,000. The full walkthrough is in my [Fish Audio API tutorial](/fish-audio-api/).

## Where it falls short

**The web app can be slow.** Generating long audio through the browser interface takes a while. The API is faster, but if you are a non-technical user who just wants to paste text and get audio, the web experience could be better.

**Emotion tags have a learning curve.** The bracket syntax accepts free-form descriptions, but placement matters more than the words. I had to experiment for about an hour before I got results I was happy with. A few example scripts with before/after audio would help.

**Voice library quality varies.** Two million voices is a lot, and not all of them are good. The search and filtering could be improved. I spent more time than I wanted browsing through mediocre voices before finding ones I liked.

**No desktop app.** It is web-only or API. If you want a native app for offline work, you are out of luck. There is a mobile app on Google Play, but I have not tested it.

Fish Audio is no longer just a voice tool. It now has a suite called **Fish Creative** in the same dashboard and account, with image generation, video generation, lip-sync, and sound effects on top of TTS and voice cloning. The [Fish Creative tutorial](https://fish.audio/blog/fish-creative-tutorial) on the Fish Audio blog covers the full set, and I wrote a longer walkthrough in [Fish Audio image and video generation](/fish-audio-image-video-generation/).

I tested the video and lip-sync side. Here is what it looks like in practice: you generate a voice (or upload your own audio), pick or generate an image of a character, and Fish Creative syncs the mouth movements to the audio. The result is a talking-head video that you can export and use directly. No After Effects, no separate lip-sync tool, no manual alignment.

## What shipped recently

Fish Audio ships updates fast. Here are the additions worth knowing about as of November 26, 2026:

**Drama-3, the new flagship.** Billed as their most emotive model, built for multi-character scenes with up to 16 speaker tags per text ([model card](https://huggingface.co/fishaudio/drama-3)). At $50 per million bytes it costs over 3x s2.1-pro, so it is a character-and-acting model, not a bulk narration model. The launch price is $10 through November 30, 2026.

**Transcribe 1 Pro (speech-to-text).** Their ASR got a Pro tier aimed at interviews, meetings, and podcasts. It marks speakers inline (`<|speaker:0|>`, `<|speaker:1|>`), keeps emotion cues like `[laughter]` in the transcript, auto-detects the language, handles recordings up to 60 minutes, and can return word-level timestamps. Same price as the standard model: $0.36 per audio hour. One footgun: the selection happens through an HTTP header, `model: transcribe-1-pro` on `POST /v1/asr`. If the header is missing or not an exact match, you get transcribe-1 instead and no error tells you. I sent the same audio to it and to AssemblyAI, so the [OpenAI Whisper alternatives test](/openai-whisper-alternatives/) has measured numbers, and my [AssemblyAI review](/assemblyai-review/) covers the other side of that test. There is also a realtime WebSocket model, `transcribe-stream-1`, billed per session hour for live voice agents.

**Pronunciation dictionaries.** If a name or acronym keeps coming out wrong, you no longer have to paste phoneme tags into every script. Build a dictionary once in the web app (or inline in the API request), publish it, and reference it by ID in TTS calls. English rules use CMU Arpabet, Chinese uses tone-number pinyin, Japanese uses romaji with pitch marks. Up to 3 dictionaries per request, 5,000 rules each.

**The MCP server does images and video now.** Fish Audio's [MCP server](https://docs.fish.audio/overview/mcp) (`https://api.fish.audio/mcp`) is not audio-only anymore. An agent connected through Claude, Cursor, or Codex can search voices, run TTS, transcribe files, and generate or edit images and videos. It signs in with OAuth and bills like the web app (plan credits, not the developer API balance), and the agent asks you to approve an image or video estimate before it generates. Video needs a Plus plan or higher.

**Voice Design.** Describe a voice in plain words (age, accent, tone, pacing) and it generates candidates, no sample audio needed. The [API version](https://docs.fish.audio/developer-guide/models-pricing/pricing-and-rate-limits) (`voice-design-1`) bills at $0.01 per successful request.

**Asset Library and Avatars.** Your uploads and generated files now sit in a central Asset Library, and you can save a character as an Avatar inside Image & Video instead of re-uploading the same face every time you lip-sync.

One more thing sitting in the docs: a full **voice-agent platform**, build an agent in a console with a prompt, voice, knowledge base, and tools, then deploy it as a web widget or on a real phone number with inbound calls, outbound dials, and warm call transfers. I have not tested it end to end yet, but it is a much bigger product than the TTS tool I originally signed up for. If you want to wire the TTS into your own agent, see [Add voice cloning TTS to Mastra with Fish Audio](/mastra-fish-audio-tts/).

## Fish Audio pricing: the real numbers

Two separate pricing systems live side by side, and most confusion comes from mixing them up.

**Web app subscriptions** (per the official plan page, annual billing is cheaper):

| Plan | Monthly | What you get |
|------|---------|--------------|
| Free | $0 | 8,000 credits/mo (about 7 minutes), 500 chars per generation, 3 public voice slots, personal use only |
| Plus | $15/mo ($11/mo annual) | 250,000 credits/mo (about 200 minutes), 15,000 chars per generation, unlimited public + 10 private voice slots, commercial use, Voice Design |
| Pro | $100/mo ($75/mo annual) | 2,000,000 credits/mo (about 1,620 minutes), 30,000 chars per generation, 3 team seats, 5 professional voice slots |

Credit math: one minute of generated audio costs roughly 600 to 625 credits. Credits reset monthly and do not roll over. The Pro tier has a 7-day money-back guarantee.

**API pricing** is pay as you go with no subscription: s2.1-pro at $15 per million UTF-8 bytes, drama-3 at $50, transcription at $0.36 per audio hour, voice design at $0.01 per request. You prepay balance and it does not expire monthly the way subscription credits do.

Which one do you pick? If you only generate audio in the browser, the subscription is the unit. If you build on the API, ignore subscriptions entirely and start with the free model; my [API tutorial](/fish-audio-api/) has the first request running in five minutes.

## Troubleshooting: when Fish Audio is not working

The fixes for the problems people actually hit:

**Generation fails or never finishes in the web app.** Long text is the usual cause. The free tier caps at 500 characters per generation, and even paid tiers cap at 15,000 to 30,000. Split the script into paragraphs and batch them. If a generation hangs, refresh and check the Asset Library before retrying; finished files sometimes land there without the UI updating.

**The API returns 429.** You hit the concurrency limit, not a rate-per-minute limit. The Starter tier allows 5 concurrent requests and there is no Retry-After header, so retry with exponential backoff. If you need more concurrency, prepaying balance unlocks the next tier immediately; you do not have to spend it first.

**Transcription comes back without speaker labels.** You are on transcribe-1, not transcribe-1-pro. The header `model: transcribe-1-pro` must match exactly; a missing or mistyped header silently falls back to the base model.

**Emotion tags do nothing.** Check which model you are calling. Bracket syntax like `[whispers]` works on s2.1-pro and s2-pro. If your integration still passes `s1`, you get the old parenthesis behavior, and s1 retires on December 31, 2026.

**The cloned voice sounds off in another language.** Clones carry accent and prosody from the source audio. Record the sample in the language you care about most, or pick a community voice native to that language instead.

**Webhook or MCP jobs do not run.** Image and video generation through MCP requires a paid plan, and the agent quotes the credit cost first. Confirm the pricing prompt instead of assuming the job started.

## Who should look elsewhere

If you only produce English content and want the absolute highest voice fidelity, ElevenLabs is still the benchmark. If you need a desktop app for offline work, Fish Audio does not have one. If you want a tool that works perfectly out of the box with no experimentation, the emotion tag system will frustrate you at first. And if you need guaranteed latency for a production voice agent, the free API model has no TTFA guarantee; budget for s2.1-pro.

## Final thoughts

I came for cheaper cloning and stayed for bracket tags and the free API. ElevenLabs has closed part of the gap since I first wrote this, so the case for Fish now rests on API price, the free model, and emotion control rather than languages or cloning cost. Clone quality is good enough for YouTube. The web app is still slower than it should be, library search is messy, and tags take practice.

The direction is clear though: drama-3 at the top end, a realtime transcription model for agents, open weights at the bottom. Fish Audio is turning into a voice platform with a TTS feature attached. If you are already paying for it, test the creative tools before you pay for a separate lip-sync service, and test the free API model before you pay for any TTS at all.
