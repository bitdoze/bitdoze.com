## What a 10-inch rack is, and what fits in it

A 10-inch rack is exactly what it says: standard 19-inch rack spacing halved, with the same 1.75-inch U height and square-hole cage-nut rails. One thing that surprises people: the RackMate ecosystem mounts with M5 cage nuts and screws, smaller than the M6 hardware your spare 19-inch rack bag is full of, so don't count your leftover nuts as compatible. The format took off after Jeff Geerling's [Project MINI RACK](https://www.jeffgeerling.com/blog/2025/project-mini-rack-compact-and-portable-homelabs/) ([GitHub](https://github.com/geerlingguy/mini-rack)) showed how much homelab fits in one.

Assembled, per DeskPi's wiki: the T1 is 281 x 200 x 451mm (W x D x H) with 212mm of internal depth, and the T2 stretches the same width to 281 x 260 x 629mm including the top handle. The T0 Plus runs the 260mm-deep frame too, so the shallow one in the family is actually the T1.

What fits: patch panels, network switches, mini PCs on shelves, Raspberry Pi mounts, small UPS units on shelves. What doesn't: anything 19-inch (standard servers, most NAS chassis, rackmount UPSes, which need the [rack-mount UPS](/best-rack-mount-ups-homelab/) guide instead), and anything deeper than about 210mm in the T1 or 260mm in the deeper frames.

A quick fit-check with real numbers, because "does it fit" is the question that decides this whole purchase. Take the 8U build below: the Mini S13 is about 115 x 102 x 39mm, so on a 1U shelf it uses under half the frame's 212mm internal depth and leaves room for cables behind it. The TL-SG105S-M2 at roughly 100 x 98 x 25mm sits beside it on the same shelf if you'd rather not spend a U on a switch. Both fit inside the ~210mm usable depth with double-digit millimeters of margin, which is what power bricks and right-angle cables need. Where people get surprised: a mini PC deeper than ~190mm, a patch panel plus a rackmounted PoE switch, or a UPS brick deeper than the frame. Measure the deepest box, add 20-30mm of cable slack, and compare against the frame's internal depth, not its marketing number.

## Power and cooling

Two things matter and neither is complicated. Power: the PDU problem above means the practical answer is a compact strip plus the shelf UPS, and keep the high-draw stuff (a NAS brick, a PoE switch) on the battery side. Cooling: open frames get a free pass on airflow, which is the format's quiet advantage over a closed cabinet. The realistic overheating list is short: PoE switches dumping 60W+ of injector heat, NAS bricks, and stacked mini PCs. In a closed cabinet that's a real problem; in an open RackMate it mostly isn't, because the whole frame is ventilation. The exception is a PoE switch in a stuffed build, which still wants a vented U of air around it. Mini PCs exhaust forward or sideways, so leave a hand's width of clearance and don't stack two boxes directly on one shelf.

## FAQ

<Accordion label="What is a 10-inch rack?" group="faq" expanded="true">
A half-width server rack: standard 19-inch spacing shrunk to 10 inches, same 1.75-inch U height. It's meant for network gear, mini PCs, and small homelab hardware, popularized by Jeff Geerling's Project MINI RACK.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="Is a 10-inch rack worth it for a homelab?" group="faq">
If your gear is loose on a shelf or desk, yes: it turns a pile of boxes into one organized unit for around $120 plus parts. If everything already has a home and stays cool, it's optional tidiness, not a requirement.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="What fits in a 10-inch rack?" group="faq">
Patch panels, switches, mini PCs and Pis on shelves, and shelf-sized UPS units. Standard 19-inch gear, most NAS chassis, and rack-mount UPSes don't fit. Usable depth runs ~210mm on the RackMate T1 and ~260mm on the T0 Plus and T2 frames.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="RackMate T1 vs T2: which should I get?" group="faq">
The T1 8U covers a normal homelab build for $120. The T2 12U adds four U for $30, which is worth it if you already know you're building past a switch plus one mini PC.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="Can I put a UPS in a 10-inch rack?" group="faq">
Not a rack-mount one; they don't exist in 10-inch. A compact tower UPS on a 1U shelf is the answer, and Eaton's desktop standby models are marketed as fitting 10-inch racks.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="How deep is a 10-inch mini rack?" group="faq">
Depends on the frame: the RackMate T1 is the shallower one at ~210mm internal depth, while the T0 Plus and T2 run 260mm-deep frames per DeskPi's wiki, and Tecmojo's are around 200mm. Measure your deepest device, usually the switch or a mini PC on a shelf, before you commit.
</Accordion>
