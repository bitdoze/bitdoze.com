## First: the JHL9480 reality check

Nearly every sub-$200 80Gbps enclosure uses Intel's JHL9480 ("Barlow Ridge") controller. Same chip, similar peak speeds. What you're actually choosing between:

- **Thermals.** Sustained writes throttle a hot SSD. Fanless aluminum works for bursty use; fans matter for hour-long transfers.
- **Backward compatibility.** TB5 enclosures should work at 40Gbps on TB4/USB4 hosts — but TB3 support is spotty (details per product below).
- **Your SSD.** The enclosure is half the cost. You need a **PCIe 4.0 x4 or faster NVMe** to break 3,000 MB/s — a Gen3 drive caps around there regardless of enclosure. And on Gen5 drives, watch power draw: OWC recommends SSDs under 11W for bus-powered use.

## Which SSD to put inside

The enclosure is half the purchase. Pair it with a midrange Gen4 NVMe — spending more on the SSD than the enclosure is the correct allocation.

| SSD | Capacity | Why | Price range |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [WD_BLACK SN850X](/go/wd-black-sn850x-2tb/) | 1TB / 2TB / 4TB | The default pick — fast, efficient, widely available | $75–$230 |
| Samsung 990 Pro | 1TB / 2TB / 4TB | Slightly faster in benchmarks, runs a bit hotter | $90–$260 |
| WD SN770 | 500GB / 1TB / 2TB | Budget option — still Gen4, ~5,000 MB/s reads | $45–$120 |

Avoid Gen5 drives in bus-powered enclosures — they draw too much power (OWC recommends ≤11W sustained) and you won't see the speed benefit since TB5's PCIe tunnel caps around 64Gbps anyway. A good Gen4 drive already saturates that.

## What speed should you actually expect?

| Host port | Realistic reads | Realistic writes | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Thunderbolt 5 / USB4 v2 | ~6,000–7,000 MB/s | ~4,000–5,000 MB/s | Needs a Gen4/5 SSD to get there |
| Thunderbolt 4 / USB4 | ~3,800 MB/s | ~3,000 MB/s | Same 40Gbps ceiling as always |
| Thunderbolt 3 | ~2,800 MB/s | ~2,500 MB/s | Only if the enclosure supports TB3 — see table above |
| USB 3.2 Gen2x2 / Gen2 | 1,000–2,000 MB/s | 800–1,500 MB/s | Fallback mode, works everywhere |

Write speeds depend on your SSD's cache and the enclosure's thermals. This is where the fanned models earn their keep on 100GB+ transfers. An 80G enclosure on a 40G port works fine (it just runs at 40G), so buying ahead of a TB5 laptop upgrade isn't wasted money.

## FAQ

<Accordion label="Do I need Thunderbolt 5 on my computer to use these?" group="faq">
No — all of these fall back to 40Gbps on TB4/USB4 ports and run fine. You just won't see the ~6,000 MB/s peak. TB3 support varies: Acasis doesn't support it at all, UGREEN needs macOS 15+, OWC and TerraMaster handle it.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="Will a Gen5 SSD make these faster?" group="faq">
Marginally, but watch power: bus-powered enclosures can't always feed a hungry Gen5 drive (OWC recommends ≤11W sustained). A good Gen4 drive like the SN850X already saturates the ~64Gbps PCIe tunnel that TB5 provides, so Gen5 is mostly wasted money here.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="Can I boot macOS or run VMs off these?" group="faq">
Yes — this is the main reason to buy 80G over a cheap 10G USB enclosure. At ~6,000 MB/s reads, a VM or a booted OS feels close to internal storage. Keep the enclosure plugged directly into the host rather than through a dock for consistent latency.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="Fanless or fan — which is better?" group="faq">
Fanless (OWC, TerraMaster) for bursty everyday use and silence. Fan (Acasis, UGREEN, RayCue) for sustained writes — if you regularly move 100GB+ files, the active cooling prevents the SSD from thermal-throttling mid-transfer.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="Why not just buy a prebuilt portable SSD?" group="faq">
You can — prebuilt TB5 drives like the LaCie Pro5 exist. But an enclosure plus your own NVMe is cheaper per TB, you pick the SSD, and when 4TB drives drop in price you can upgrade the enclosure's capacity instead of replacing the whole unit.
</Accordion>
