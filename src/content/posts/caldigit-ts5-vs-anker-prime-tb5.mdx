The **CalDigit TS5** ($399.95) and the **Anker Prime TB5** ($347.86 at last check) are the two Thunderbolt 5 docks people actually cross-shop — same 140W charging, same 2.5GbE, both under $400 while the rest of the field sits at $500+. They get there in opposite ways: the TS5 is a fanless aluminum heatsink with three downstream TB5 ports, and the Anker is a compact actively-cooled cube with a shared HDMI/DP output.

Short version: the TS5 is the safer pick for most desks. The Anker makes sense if you want the smaller box, you run Windows or macOS on a TB4/TB5 host, and $50 matters. Compatibility is the deciding factor — details below.

## Where the TS5 wins

**Downstream bandwidth.** Three TB5 ports at 80Gbps each means you can hang a fast NVMe enclosure, a second display chain, and a TB4 peripheral without splitting one port's bandwidth. The Anker has two downstream TB5 — fine for most desks, tighter if you daisy-chain storage.

**Display flexibility.** The TS5 gives you DisplayPort 2.1 plus full video over any downstream TB5 port: dual 6K@60Hz on macOS, dual 8K@60Hz or triple 4K@144Hz on Windows TB5 hosts. The Anker has one dedicated display output that's *either* HDMI 2.1 *or* DP 2.1 — check which version of the SKU you're buying — and the rest of your monitors go through the TB5 ports. Fewer options, more cable-planning.

**Card readers.** UHS-II on both SD and microSD. If you offload camera media, that's ~300MB/s vs the Anker's slower reader.

**Compatibility.** This is the big one. The TS5 works on Thunderbolt 3, TB4, USB4, and USB-C hosts with reduced functionality, and it doesn't care whether your OS is macOS, Windows, Linux, or ChromeOS. The Anker Prime TB5 explicitly does **not** support Linux, ChromeOS, or Thunderbolt 3/4 Windows hosts — it's a TB5-on-Windows-or-Mac product. For a homelab crowd that occasionally docks a Linux laptop, that alone settles it.

**No fan.** The aluminum chassis is the heatsink. Silent, and one less moving part to fail.

## Where the Anker wins

**Price.** $347.86 vs $399.95 — roughly $52 cheaper at current pricing, and Anker discounts more aggressively during sale events.

**Footprint.** It's a compact cube that sits vertically; the TS5 is a flat slab that wants desk space. If your setup is tight, the Anker is easier to place.

**240W cable included.** The box ships with a proper 240W-rated TB5 cable. Minor, but real.

**USB-A count.** Three USB-A ports vs the TS5's two (one of which is a slow 480Mbps port meant for keyboards). If your desk is still mostly USB-A peripherals, the Anker layout is friendlier.

## The fan question

The Anker's active cooling fan is the trade-off for its compact size. Under sustained load — big file copies to a TB5 SSD plus displays — it spins up and stays audible. The TS5 is passively cooled and runs warm to the touch by design; CalDigit explicitly tells you to leave clearance around it. Neither is a problem, but if the dock sits next to your microphone, the fanless one is the right call.

## Reliability signals

The TS5 has 80 reviews at 4.2★ — modest but real. The Anker has 6 reviews at 4.4★, which tells you almost nothing yet; it's a newer listing. CalDigit's warranty reputation in the dock market is the stronger of the two brands historically. If early-adopter risk bothers you, that tilts toward the TS5 as well.

## What about the TS5 Plus?

For $100 more, the [CalDigit TS5 Plus](/go/caldigit-ts5-plus/) ($499.99, 4.2★/277) swaps the 2.5GbE for **10GbE**, bumps the PSU to 330W, adds ports to reach 20 total (5x USB-A + 5x USB-C, all 10Gbps), and pushes 36W to downstream ports for charging hungrier devices. One caveat: it has 3 TB5 ports *total* rather than the TS5's 4, and Windows users need to install a driver for the 10GbE. If you're wiring into a 10GbE NAS or switch, the Plus earns its premium. Otherwise the plain TS5 is the better value.

## FAQ

<Accordion label="Does the Anker Prime TB5 work with Thunderbolt 4 laptops?" group="faq">
On Windows and macOS, yes — Anker supports TB4 hosts. What it does not support is Thunderbolt 3, Linux, or ChromeOS. If any of those are in your future, buy the CalDigit instead.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="Can either dock charge a 16-inch MacBook Pro at full speed?" group="faq">
Both deliver 140W to the host, which is what the M-series Pro/Max 16" draws. You're covered either way; the difference is the Anker includes the 240W-rated cable while CalDigit bundles a 1m braided TB5 cable.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="Is the Anker's fan loud?" group="faq">
It's audible under sustained load — not coil-whine annoying, but present. The dock is compact and actively cooled, which is the price of the small footprint. If silence matters, the fanless TS5 is the answer.
</Accordion>

<Accordion label="Do these work with two monitors on a Mac?" group="faq">
Both support dual 6K@60Hz on Apple Silicon that can drive two displays. Base M1/M2/M3 chips are still limited to one external display regardless of dock — that's a laptop limitation, not a dock one.
</Accordion>
