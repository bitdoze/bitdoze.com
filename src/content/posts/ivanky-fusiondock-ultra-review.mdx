## What You Get in the Box

The FusionDock Ultra arrives with everything you need to get started:

- The dock itself
- A dual USB-C magnetic cable (1.2m) that clicks together for MacBook Pro or splits apart for Mac Mini/Studio
- A separate Thunderbolt 5 / USB-C 80Gbps cable (1.2m)
- A 240W power adapter with power cord
- User manual, quick start guide, and FAQs booklet

The magnetic cable is a nice touch. On a MacBook Pro, the two connectors snap together magnetically so you plug them in as one unit. On a Mac Mini or Mac Studio, you peel them apart to reach the spaced-out Thunderbolt ports. Simple and it works well.

One thing to note: the [iVANKY official store](https://ivanky.com/products/fusiondock-ultra) sells an "80Gbps Cable Kit" bundle that is exclusive to their store. Check the Amazon listing's included-items section before assuming the cable set matches. The contents can differ between retailers.

## Design and Build

The dock has a solid aluminum chassis. iVANKY says they use a 2,000-ton extrusion press to form the body, and it feels like it. The thing is dense and does not slide around on the desk. There is a copper-alloy midframe inside that helps pull heat away from the chips, and the chassis floats slightly to let air pass through.

On the front: two USB-A ports, seven USB-C ports (one delivers 45W PD), a 3.5mm combo audio jack, and UHS-II SD and microSD card slots. There is also a small iVANKY logo that lights up when the dock is powered on. No power button, which means it turns on and off with your Mac.

On the back: two USB-C host ports (80/120Gbps) for connecting to your Mac, four USB-C downstream ports (80/120Gbps), one DisplayPort 2.1, one HDMI 2.0, one 10Gb Ethernet, optical audio, two more USB-A ports, and a Kensington lock slot.

<Notice type="info" title="Apple Silicon Only">
The FusionDock Ultra is designed for Apple Silicon Macs only. It will not work with Intel Macs, Windows PCs, or Chromebooks. You need macOS 15.1 or later. Apple Silicon MacBook Air with USB-C ports is also supported, not just MacBook Pro and Mac Mini.
</Notice>

iVANKY backs the dock with a 24-month warranty and a 14-day money-back window when purchased from their official store. At this price, that recourse matters.

## Using It With One Cable vs. Two

One thing that confused me at first: the dock has two host ports, and you can use it with just one of them if you do not need all 26 ports. When you plug into the bottom host port only, you get access to the lower chip's ports, which still gives you two USB-C monitor outputs, the Ethernet port, the USB-A ports, and everything on the front. That is how I have been running it most days: one cable to my M4 Pro Mac Mini, two monitors, an SSD, and Ethernet. No issues.

If you want the full bandwidth and all display outputs, connect both host ports with the included magnetic cable. That activates the second chip and unlocks the upper row of display-capable ports for quad display support.

One caveat on the 80/120Gbps port speeds: the 120Gbps mode only kicks in with a USB4 120Gb/s-capable host, such as the 14-inch MacBook Pro with M4 Pro or M4 Max (2024). On other Macs, those ports run at 80Gbps. Both are plenty for external drives and displays, but do not expect 120Gbps on an M1 or M2 machine.

## Dual-Chip Architecture: Why It Matters

Most Thunderbolt docks use a single chip to handle everything: displays, ports, power delivery, and data. When you push that chip hard (four monitors plus an SSD plus Ethernet), things can slow down or get unstable.

The FusionDock Ultra has two chips inside. Each handles its own set of ports and display outputs, so the workload is split. In practice, I did not notice any speed drops when running two 4K monitors (one OLED, one at 144Hz) while also connected to an external NVMe enclosure via one of the 80/120Gbps downstream USB-C ports with a Thunderbolt 5 enclosure. Read and write speeds on the SSD hovered around 6 GB/s, which is about what I expect from a [Thunderbolt 5 NVMe enclosure](/best-tb5-nvme-enclosures/) on this class of port. The monitors did not cause any bandwidth dip.

That 6 GB/s figure is only possible on the 80/120Gbps ports with a TB5 enclosure. If you connect the same drive to one of the 10Gbps USB-C or USB-A ports, you will see around 1 GB/s, a hard ceiling from the port's bandwidth, not the drive.

## Cooling and Noise

The dock has two active fans: one pulls air in, the other pushes it out through the aluminum chassis. I have been sitting next to it for two weeks and I can say with certainty that it is quiet. It is noticeably quieter than my previous [ASUS Master Thunderbolt 5 Dock DC510](/asus-thunderbolt-5-dock-dc510-review/), which sounded like a small jet engine whenever I pushed it. The FusionDock Ultra's fan noise is comparable to my M4 Pro Mac Mini. Sometimes the Mini is louder under load.

iVANKY publishes a noise figure of 44-46 dBA measured at 1 cm in a quiet room. MacRumors independently described it as "audible but more of a low white noise," which matches my impression. At normal desk distance you will not notice it.

Under light use, the fans can turn off entirely. Under sustained heavy loads (SSD transfers plus two monitors), they ramp up but stay below what I consider distracting in a home office. The chassis gets warm to the touch but never hot.

## How to Verify Your 10GbE and SSD Speeds

Most dock reviews stop at "it works." Here is how to confirm your setup is actually running at the speeds you paid for. These commands work on macOS.

### Confirm 10GbE Negotiated 10G

Open **System Information** → **Ethernet** and look for "10 Gigabit" under the link speed. Or from Terminal:

```bash
ifconfig en0 | grep media
```

You want to see `10GbaseT` in the output. If you see `1000baseT` or lower, the problem is the cable or the switch, not the dock.

To actually push throughput, run `iperf3` against a 10GbE NAS or another 10Gb host on your network:

```bash
iperf3 -c <NAS-IP> -t 30
```

Expect somewhere around 900 MB/s+ at the application layer. If you see much less, check the cable (use Cat6a or better) and the switch port.

### Confirm Active Ports and Chips

Open **System Information** → **Thunderbolt/USB4**. The tree shows both host links and what is connected to each chip's port row. This is how you verify that single-cable mode is giving you the ports you expect, and which chip is driving which displays.

### Benchmark the SSD on the Right Port

Use Blackmagic Disk Speed Test or AmorphousDiskMark against the specific port where the drive is connected. The number you see depends entirely on the port:

- **80/120Gbps downstream USB-C port + TB5 NVMe enclosure:** up to ~6 GB/s (this is where the Acasis TB501-class drives live)
- **20Gbps port:** around 2 GB/s
- **10Gbps port:** around 1 GB/s, a hard ceiling from the port, not the drive

<Notice type="info" title="Why This Matters">
A 10Gbps USB-C port physically cannot exceed about 1.25 GB/s. If you see 6 GB/s in a benchmark, you are on a Thunderbolt 5 port with a TB5 enclosure. If you see ~1 GB/s on a fast drive, check which port it is plugged into before blaming the drive or the dock.
</Notice>

## What Could Be Better

No product is perfect. A few things I noticed:

- **No built-in NVMe SSD slot.** You can plug in an external enclosure, but some competing docks have a slot right on the board. If you want internal storage, you need an external enclosure.
- **Apple Silicon only.** If you have a work-issued Windows laptop or an older Intel Mac, this dock will not work for you. The dual-chip design is specifically built for Apple's display architecture.
- **No power button.** The dock powers on when your Mac wakes up and off when it sleeps. Some people prefer manual control.
- **No firmware update channel.** iVANKY's Download Center offers only a "Monitor Resolution Detect Software." There are no dock firmware downloads. If a bug is found in the dock's firmware, your only recourse is to wait for a hardware revision or live with it. CalDigit's docks are firmware-updatable, which is worth knowing if you are comparing.
- **HDMI is 2.0, not 2.1.** The HDMI port caps out at roughly 4K@60. For high-refresh 4K or 6K output, use the DisplayPort 2.1 or one of the USB-C downstream ports.
- **The price.** At $599.99 on Amazon during Black Friday (list $749.99, everyday price $649.99), this is not a casual purchase. You need to actually use most of these ports to justify it. My buy threshold is under $550. That is where the value math works. In Europe, expect around €700 (Tweakers listed it at that price in June 2026). ## What should you know about frequently Asked Questions?

<Accordion label="Is the FusionDock Ultra compatible with Windows PCs?" group="fusiondock-faq">

<Accordion label="Can I use the dock with just one cable?" group="fusiondock-faq">
Yes. Plugging into the lower host port gives you access to the bottom chip's ports, which includes two USB-C display outputs, Ethernet, USB-A, and all front-facing ports. You only need the second cable if you want more displays or full quad-display bandwidth. If the dock stops waking your displays after a sleep cycle, replug the host cable and check for macOS updates. <Accordion label="Does the dock work with Mac Mini and Mac Studio?" group="fusiondock-faq">
